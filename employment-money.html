SCRA 6% Interest Protection

⚖️ YOUR RIGHT: 50 U.S.C. §3937 can limit interest to 6% per year on a qualifying obligation incurred before the servicemember entered qualifying military service when military service materially affects the ability to pay at the pre-service rate. Interest above 6% is forgiven rather than postponed. [124]

  • Qualifying person: SCRA status depends on qualifying military service under the Act. Veteran status by itself is not enough.
  • Qualifying debt: The obligation generally must have been incurred before the qualifying military-service period.
  • Mortgage-type obligation: the §3937 cap generally applies during military service and for one year afterward.
  • Other covered obligation: the §3937 cap generally applies during military service.
  • Creditor relief: a creditor may ask a court for relief from the cap if the servicemember's ability to pay above 6% is not materially affected by military service.

⚠️ DEADLINE: To invoke §3937 through the ordinary notice route, provide the creditor written notice and a copy of military orders or another appropriate indicator of military service no later than 180 days after termination or release from that military service. [171]

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE: Send the request in a form you can prove was delivered and keep the orders/service documentation, account statements, pre-service contract, and creditor response.

“I am requesting the interest-rate protection in 50 U.S.C. §3937 for account [NUMBER]. I entered qualifying military service on [DATE]. The obligation was incurred on [DATE]. I am attaching [orders / other qualifying service documentation]. Please review the account under §3937, apply any required reduction from the date required by law, and provide the adjusted balance and interest calculation in writing.”

📚 AUTHORITY: 50 U.S.C. §3937 and current DOJ SCRA guidance. [124] [171]

Other SCRA problem? Foreclosure, eviction, lease termination, repossession, default judgment, and litigation stays use different SCRA provisions. Housing issues should also be checked against the Housing guide rather than treating the 6% rule as a universal SCRA remedy.

FDCPA Debt Validation: First Identify the Collector

⚖️ YOUR RIGHT: The federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act does not automatically apply to every creditor or every debt. The validation rules in 15 U.S.C. §1692g apply to covered debt collectors. An original creditor collecting its own debt is not automatically a covered debt collector merely because it asks for payment. [43]

Who contacted you?What to verify
Original creditorDo not assume §1692g applies. Check the creditor, account, contract, and other consumer-protection rules.
Collection agency / covered debt collectorReview the validation notice and its statutory dispute period.
Debt buyer / servicerCoverage depends on the entity's role and when/how it obtained the debt; identify the entity before invoking FDCPA rights.

⚠️ DEADLINE: Section 1692g gives a consumer a 30-day validation period tied to receipt of the validation notice. If the consumer disputes the debt in writing within that period, the collector must cease collection of the disputed debt until it mails the verification required by the statute. Without a qualifying written dispute, lawful collection activity can continue during the validation period so long as it does not overshadow or contradict the validation rights. [43] [93]

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE: Preserve the validation notice and envelope/electronic delivery record. A practical request can ask for an itemization and account information, but do not claim the FDCPA requires every document you choose to request.

“I dispute the debt identified in your validation notice and request the verification required by 15 U.S.C. §1692g. Please also provide, if available, an itemization of the amount and information identifying the original creditor/account. Any additional documents I request are practical requests and are not represented here as documents the FDCPA necessarily requires in every case.”

If collection conduct appears unlawful, preserve calls, letters, voicemails, texts, account records, and the validation notice. CFPB accepts consumer complaints, and private legal remedies may exist depending on the facts. [98]

TDIU: Service-Connected Disabilities and Substantially Gainful Employment

⚖️ YOUR RIGHT: TDIU can pay at the 100% compensation rate when service-connected disabilities prevent a veteran from securing and following substantially gainful employment. [26]

Schedular path — §4.16(a): The regulation includes the familiar percentage framework: one disability rated 60% or more, or multiple disabilities with at least one rated 40% or more and a combined rating of 70% or more, with regulatory grouping rules. Marginal employment is not substantially gainful employment under the regulation. [163]

Do not stop there — §4.16(b): A veteran who cannot secure and follow a substantially gainful occupation because of service-connected disabilities may still require extraschedular TDIU consideration even if the §4.16(a) percentages are not met. The regulation directs rating boards to submit qualifying cases for extraschedular consideration. [163]

Marginal/protected work: Earnings and the facts of the work environment matter. Do not assume that any paid work automatically defeats TDIU, or that any “sheltered” job automatically establishes entitlement.

📄 FORMS: VA Form 21-8940 is the Veteran's TDIU application; VA Form 21-4192 requests employment information. [24] [25]

📋 EVIDENCE: Work history, earnings, accommodations, attendance, employer records, medical evidence, vocational evidence, and evidence connecting work limitations to service-connected conditions can be useful. Not every item is legally mandatory in every case.

If VA denies TDIU, use the Appeals guide and preserve the decision letter and deadline.

Veterans Pension: Do Not Use One Service Rule for Everyone

⚖️ ELIGIBILITY: Veterans Pension is a needs-based benefit for qualifying wartime Veterans who also meet discharge, income/net-worth, and age/disability requirements. [165]

  • Entered active duty before September 8, 1980: VA's current guidance includes the 90-days-active-duty / at-least-one-wartime-day pathway, with other statutory wartime-service pathways also possible.
  • Entered active duty after September 7, 1980: minimum-active-duty requirements can include 24 months or the full period called/ordered to active duty, subject to exceptions, plus qualifying wartime service.
  • Age/disability condition: current VA eligibility can be met through age 65+, permanent and total disability, qualifying nursing-home status, or receipt of SSDI/SSI, in addition to the other requirements.
  • Income/net worth: current limits and countable-income rules apply; verify the current VA figures rather than relying on an old dollar amount.

📚 AUTHORITY: 38 U.S.C. §1521 and current VA Pension eligibility guidance. [164] [165]

📄 FORM: VA Form 21P-527EZ is the current Veterans Pension application. VA Form 21-2680 may support Aid and Attendance / Housebound claims when applicable. [45] [49]

Aid and Attendance / Housebound can change the applicable pension rate; it does not create a universal exception to every service, discharge, income, or net-worth requirement.

Unemployment Compensation for Ex-Servicemembers (UCX)

Eligible former servicemembers may receive UCX through state unemployment systems. Benefit amount, duration, weekly certification, waiting periods, and appeal procedure depend substantially on the state. [94]

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE: File with the state unemployment agency promptly after separation, keep the DD-214/service records and claim confirmation, and use the state's actual denial/hearing notice if a dispute arises. Do not copy another state's appeal deadline.

GI Bill / Education Debt

Education overpayments require a debt-type check before you choose a response. Current VA guidance distinguishes amounts for which a school may be financially liable—such as certain tuition/fee or Yellow Ribbon overpayments—from student debts such as housing, books, or other allowances. [166]

  • Preserve the VA debt letter and the school's enrollment certifications/changes.
  • Ask the school certifying official to correct a certification error when the school record is wrong.
  • If VA says you owe the debt, use the VA Debt decision tree to distinguish dispute, waiver, underlying decision review, and repayment.
  • Do not use a generic “30-day GI Bill waiver deadline.” The controlling notice and debt type matter.

Income Protection Evidence Checklist

  • SCRA: orders, service dates, account statements, original contract/debt date, creditor correspondence, interest calculations.
  • FDCPA: validation notice, envelope/electronic delivery record, letters, call logs, voicemails, payment/account records.
  • TDIU: VA decisions, rating information, employment history, earnings, accommodations, medical/vocational evidence.
  • Pension: discharge/service records, income/net-worth documents, medical/nursing-home/SSDI/SSI evidence when relevant.
  • Education debt: debt notice, enrollment certifications, withdrawal/change records, school and VA correspondence.

These are practical evidence lists, not statements that every item is legally required in every case.
