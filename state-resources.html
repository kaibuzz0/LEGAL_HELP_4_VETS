How to Use This Directory

This directory points you to state veterans affairs offices, legal aid, and VSO contacts. Use it as a starting point, not the final answer. Programs, funding, and phone numbers change.

  1. Start with your state veterans affairs office (usually under the governor's office or state military department).
  2. Ask for: veterans service officer contacts, state benefit applications, legal aid referrals, and veterans treatment court information.
  3. Verify any URL or phone number before relying on it. If a link is marked pending, call the state office directly.
  4. Keep a call log of who you spoke with, what they promised, and any case numbers.

National Hotlines (Regardless of State)

  • Veterans Crisis Line: 988, press 1 [1]
  • Homeless Veterans Hotline: 1-877-4AID-VET [32]
  • SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357 [2]
  • VA Benefits: 1-800-827-1000

What This Is and Who Qualifies

This directory lists state and territorial veteran resources: state veterans affairs offices, VA regional benefit offices, veterans homes, medical centers, and notes about local legal aid and veterans treatment courts. State programs change often and rules differ by state, so verify everything locally. [95] [15]

Who qualifies: Any veteran, servicemember, family member, or advocate looking for state-specific benefits, local VSO contacts, or in-state services. Residency rules and eligibility requirements vary. [15]

How to Use This Directory

  1. Find your state or territory below. Read the status tags: “verified” means the URL loaded successfully from this environment; “pending” means the link was assembled from public information but not verified at build time. [15]
  2. Call your state veterans affairs office and ask for the specific benefit you need: property tax exemption, tuition program, employment preference, veterans home, etc. [95]
  3. Ask the county veteran service officer (CVSO) for local forms and deadlines. Many benefits are administered at the county level.
  4. Verify veterans treatment court availability with the local court clerk or district attorney; do not assume a court exists based on this directory. [6]
  5. If a link is broken, search the agency name plus your state, or call the national VA number above.

Document Checklist for State Benefits

  • DD-214 (preferably member copy 4)
  • State ID or driver’s license showing residence
  • VA disability rating letter (for tax exemptions and tuition waivers)
  • Income and asset documents (for veterans home or pension supplements)
  • Marriage and birth certificates if dependents are included
  • Copies of applications and receipts with tracking numbers

What If a State Says No

  • Ask for the denial in writing with the specific statute or regulation cited.
  • Contact your county veteran service officer: they often know workarounds and appeal paths. [13]
  • Escalate to your state representative or senator: state legislators can often get answers faster than phone queues.
  • Federal benefits: Many needs can also be met through VA regardless of state denial. Use the other pages in this site.

Always confirm the current URL and services with the state office before acting. State names, agencies, and phone numbers change.
