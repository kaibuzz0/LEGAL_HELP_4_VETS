Build a VA Disability Claim: File, Document, and Protect Your Date

DO THIS FIRST

Last legally verified: 2026-08-27
Primary authorities checked: 38 CFR § 3.155 · 38 CFR § 3.400 · current VA Form 21-0966 guidance
Forms checked: VA Form 21-0966 · VA Form 21-526EZ
Warning: Intent-to-File treatment differs by claim type and does not apply to Supplemental Claims under 38 CFR § 3.155(b).

Start the claim correctly

  1. If you are still gathering evidence, determine whether an Intent to File is available for your type of claim.
  2. Get your DD-214, service treatment records, personnel records, and relevant private/VA medical records.
  3. Identify each condition and the theory of service connection you are claiming.
  4. Submit the correct complete claim form or current VA online application.
  5. Save a complete copy and proof of submission.
⚠️ DEADLINE

Intent to File — use it precisely

An Intent to File can help establish an earlier potential effective date when VA receives a complete qualifying claim within the required period. Under 38 CFR § 3.155(b), if VA receives the complete qualifying application within one year after a valid Intent to File, VA generally treats the complete claim as filed on the Intent-to-File date, subject to the effective-date rules that apply to the benefit.

📄 FORM VA Form 21-0966 is the current paper Intent to File form for compensation, pension, survivors pension, and DIC. [22]

Do not describe an Intent to File as automatically “locking” an effective date in every case. Effective-date rules contain exceptions, and the Intent-to-File rule does not apply to Supplemental Claims.

📚 AUTHORITY 38 CFR §§ 3.155, 3.400.

📋 CHECKLIST

Evidence that usually matters

  • DD-214 and service dates
  • Service treatment and personnel records
  • Current diagnosis or evidence of current disability where required
  • Evidence of an in-service event, injury, disease, exposure, or aggravation
  • Medical nexus evidence when the connection is not established by a presumption or other rule
  • Lay statements describing observable symptoms, events, and functional impact
  • Employment evidence if work impairment matters to the benefit or rating issue
  • VA and private treatment records

A negative medical opinion does not automatically end the claim

⚖️ YOUR RIGHT A negative medical opinion is evidence VA may weigh; it is not a rule that automatically terminates a claim. Depending on the facts and review stage, an opinion may be challenged or outweighed by other competent evidence.

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE Review whether the opinion:

  • used an accurate factual premise;
  • addressed relevant service and medical records;
  • addressed competent lay evidence;
  • gave a reasoned medical explanation;
  • was within the examiner’s competency and scope;
  • considered contrary medical evidence;
  • performed the required testing or examination;
  • complied with a remand instruction, if a remand applied.

A defect does not guarantee a new examination or a favorable result. Identify the specific problem and use the authorized review process that fits your decision.

C&P exam: describe the whole condition accurately

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE Be honest, specific, and consistent. Describe the full severity, frequency, duration, and functional impact of your symptoms. Include what happens on bad or flare-up days without exaggerating or minimizing your ordinary condition.

  • Bring or review a symptom timeline if it helps you remember accurately.
  • Explain effects on work, sleep, movement, concentration, relationships, and daily activities when relevant.
  • Do not guess at dates or symptoms you do not remember.
  • If you believe the examination was inadequate, identify the concrete deficiency rather than simply demanding another exam.

[28]

No informal “protective claim” substitute

The current claims system uses prescribed filing methods. Do not rely on a homemade “protective claim” letter as a substitute for the valid Intent-to-File methods in 38 CFR § 3.155(b) or for a required complete claim form.

📄 FORM For a qualifying Intent to File, use the current VA online method, designated oral process, or prescribed VA Form 21-0966 as allowed by the regulation. [22]

If VA denies the claim

⚠️ DEADLINE Read the actual decision notice and its review-rights instructions. Do not use a generic protective-appeal letter. Use the authorized Supplemental Claim, Higher-Level Review, or Board Appeal process that applies. Open the Appeals guide.

  • New and relevant evidence: consider Supplemental Claim (VA Form 20-0995).
  • Existing-record error or difference of opinion: consider Higher-Level Review (VA Form 20-0996) if eligible; no new evidence.
  • Veterans Law Judge review: consider Board Appeal (VA Form 10182).
📋 CHECKLIST

Proof to keep

  • Intent-to-File confirmation, if used
  • Complete claim submission
  • Every medical and lay attachment
  • VA.gov confirmation or mailing/fax receipt
  • C&P appointment notices and exam records when available
  • Every VA decision notice and review-rights enclosure
YOUR NEXT ACTION: If you are still gathering evidence, determine whether a valid Intent to File applies to your claim type. Then use the current VA process and keep proof of every submission.
