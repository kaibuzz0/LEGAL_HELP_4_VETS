CLAIMS BATTLE ROOM

VA disability compensation pays monthly tax-free benefits for service-connected disabilities. This section tells you how to file a claim from zero, what evidence wins, and what to do if VA says no.

Step-by-Step: File a Claim from Zero

  1. Preserve your effective date. Submit VA Form 21-0966 (Intent to File) online, by phone, or in writing. This locks an earlier effective date while you gather evidence. [22]
  2. Get your service records. Request your DD-214 and Service Treatment Records (STRs) from the National Personnel Records Center (NPRC) or via milConnect if still active access.
  3. Identify every condition. List physical, mental, and secondary conditions. Include conditions that developed after service if they are caused or aggravated by a service-connected condition.
  4. Gather private medical records. Ask every doctor and hospital that treated you to send records to VA or upload them yourself.
  5. Get a nexus opinion if needed. A private doctor or VA examiner must connect your current condition to service. See the nexus section below.
  6. Write lay and buddy statements. Use VA Form 21-4138 for your statement and ask family, friends, or fellow servicemembers to describe what they witnessed.
  7. File VA Form 21-526EZ. File online at VA.gov, by mail, in person, or with a VSO. [17]
  8. Track everything. Save copies, confirmation numbers, and a call log.

Evidence Checklist (Priority Order)

  1. DD-214 (characterization, dates, awards, MOS)
  2. Service Treatment Records (in-service complaints, diagnoses, treatments)
  3. Private medical records (current diagnosis and treatment history)
  4. Nexus letter / medical opinion connecting current condition to service
  5. Lay statements (VA Form 21-4138) describing symptoms and in-service events [18]
  6. Buddy statements from fellow servicemembers who witnessed the injury/event
  7. Employment records showing impact on work (especially for TDIU)
  8. VA treatment notes and C&P exam results

Nexus Letter: What It Is and How to Get One

A nexus letter is a medical opinion that states it is at least as likely as not (50% or greater probability) that your current condition is related to military service.

Who can write it: Any physician, psychologist, or specialist who reviewed your records and can give an independent opinion. A doctor who treated you is fine; a specialist in the condition is stronger.

Exact language to ask for:

"Based on my review of the veteran's service treatment records, post-service medical records, and [examination/interview], it is my medical opinion that [current diagnosed condition] is at least as likely as not related to [specific in-service injury/event/exposure]. My rationale is [specific medical reasoning, supported by records]."

Sample nexus paragraph:

"The veteran's current left knee osteoarthritis is at least as likely as not related to the documented fall and knee strain during training at Fort Benning in June 2008. In-service records note complaints of swelling and instability. Post-service imaging confirms degenerative changes consistent with the mechanism of injury described in service."

"They Said I Don't Have Enough Medical Proof"

This is the most common reason for denial. Here is how to fight it:

  1. Request Service Treatment Records (STRs): Use VA Form 20-10206 to request your complete C-file and STRs. [23]
  2. Get a private nexus opinion: A private doctor can review your records and write the medical connection VA says is missing.
  3. Write a lay statement that fills gaps: Use VA Form 21-4138 to describe, in your own words: what happened in service, symptoms since then, and how the condition affects you now. Be specific about dates, locations, and witnesses.
  4. Use the VA duty to assist argument: VA must help obtain relevant records. If VA failed to get records you identified, argue the duty to assist was not satisfied. [37]
  5. Sample language for a statement or appeal: "The veteran's lay statement, combined with the in-service event documented in [record], supports a finding that the current [condition] is at least as likely as not related to service. VA's duty to assist requires it to obtain the [missing record] identified by the veteran."

Secondary Conditions

A secondary condition is a disability caused or aggravated by a service-connected condition. Examples: knee injury causing back strain; PTSD causing sleep apnea; medication side effects causing organ damage.

How to claim: File a new claim for the secondary condition, clearly stating the primary service-connected condition that caused it. Attach a medical nexus opinion and treatment records.

Sample argument: "The veteran's service-connected left ankle instability has caused compensatory gait changes, resulting in degenerative disc disease of the lumbar spine. Dr. [Name] confirms the causal relationship in the attached nexus letter dated [date]."

TDIU (Total Disability Individual Unemployability)

TDIU pays at the 100% rate if your service-connected disabilities prevent you from maintaining substantially gainful employment, even if your combined rating is below 100%. [26]

How to file: Submit VA Form 21-8940 (Veteran's Application for Increased Compensation Based on Unemployability) and VA Form 21-4192 (Request for Employment Information) for each employer. [24] [25]

Employer letter template:

"To Whom It May Concern: [Veteran Name] worked for [Employer] from [date] to [date] as [position]. Due to [service-connected conditions], the veteran was unable to maintain [specific job requirements, attendance, productivity]. [He/She] left employment on [date]. [Signature, title, contact]."

PACT Act / Presumptive Conditions

The PACT Act adds presumptive conditions for toxic exposure, including burn pits, Agent Orange, radiation, and contaminated water. [27]

Major presumptives include: asthma, rhinitis, sinusitis, certain cancers, hypertension, Parkinson's disease, ischemic heart disease, and others depending on exposure era and location.

How to claim under PACT: If you served in a covered location/time and have a presumptive diagnosis, file VA Form 21-526EZ and state "PACT Act presumptive condition." You generally do not need a nexus letter because the law presumes the connection.

Exposure evidence to submit: DD-214 showing location/MOS, deployment records, unit records, burn pit registry participation, buddy statements about exposure, and any medical records confirming the diagnosis.

Protective Claims (Preserve Back Pay)

A protective claim is an informal communication to VA that shows intent to file a claim for a specific benefit. It can preserve an earlier effective date.

Sample protective claim letter: "I intend to file a claim for service connection for [condition]. I will submit the formal application and supporting evidence as soon as possible. [Name, SSN, signature, date]."

Send it to the VA Evidence Intake Center by certified mail or upload it to VA.gov. Then follow up with VA Form 21-526EZ within one year.

C&P Exam Prep

VA may schedule a compensation and pension (C&P) exam to evaluate your claimed condition. [28]

What to bring: ID, list of current medications, private medical records, symptom diary, and a one-page summary of your condition.

What to say: Be honest, specific, and consistent. Describe your worst days, not your best. Explain how the condition affects work, sleep, relationships, and daily tasks.

What not to say: Do not exaggerate, do not minimize, do not guess at dates. If you do not know, say "I don't remember exactly."

If the exam is inadequate: Request a new exam in writing. Point out missing tests, incorrect assumptions, or failure to review records.

Denied? Appeals Lane Decision Tree

  • Have new and relevant evidence? → Supplemental Claim (VA Form 20-0995). [19]
  • Think VA made a clear error? → Higher-Level Review (VA Form 20-0996). No new evidence. [20]
  • Want a judge and can wait? → Board Appeal (VA Form 10182). Choose direct review, evidence submission, or hearing. [21]
  • Deadline: Generally one year from the decision date to preserve the earliest effective date. Do not miss it.
YOUR NEXT ACTION: If you have not filed an intent to file, do it today at VA.gov or by calling 1-800-827-1000. Then start gathering your DD-214, STRs, and private records.
