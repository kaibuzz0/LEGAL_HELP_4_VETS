Build a VA Disability Claim

Last legally verified: 2026-08-27
Primary/current sources checked: 38 CFR §§3.155, 3.400; current VA Form 21-526EZ; VA disability evidence guidance; VA Form 20-10206; current TDIU Forms 21-8940 and 21-4192.
Important: the current VA evidence page recognizes medical and lay evidence; the evidence needed depends on the claim type.
📋 CHECKLIST

File a claim from zero

  1. Determine whether an Intent to File applies. If you need time to gather evidence, use a valid method only when your claim type is eligible.
  2. Identify the claim type: original/direct service connection, increased rating, secondary service connection, presumptive claim, TDIU, §1151, or another special claim.
  3. Get the core records: DD-214/separation documents, service treatment/personnel records, and relevant VA/private medical records.
  4. Match evidence to the legal element: current disability, in-service event/exposure/aggravation, nexus, current severity, secondary link, or work impact—depending on the claim.
  5. Use current VA Form 21-526EZ or the current online claim process.
  6. Add any required supporting forms.
  7. Save the entire submission and proof VA received it.
  8. Track later evidence, exams, and decisions.
⚠️ DEADLINE

Intent to File — use it precisely

An Intent to File can help establish an earlier potential effective date when VA receives a complete qualifying claim within the required period. Under 38 CFR §3.155(b), if VA receives the qualifying complete application within one year after a valid Intent to File, VA generally treats the complete claim as filed on the Intent-to-File date, subject to governing effective-date rules.

📄 FORM VA Form 21-0966 is the paper Intent to File form for specified benefits. The rule does not apply to Supplemental Claims.

No homemade “protective claim” substitute.

VA's current evidence guidance lists DD-214/separation documents, service treatment records, medical evidence, and lay evidence among the common claim-supporting materials.

Get missing records without confusing the deadline

📄 FORM VA Form 20-10206 can request personal compensation/pension records, including a C-file, claim examinations, DD-214, personnel file, and service treatment records.

  • Request records early if they are needed to understand the case.
  • A record request is not a disability claim and does not substitute for a required claim or review form.
  • Do not wait for a C-file if waiting would cause you to miss a filing deadline.
  • Keep your own chronological index of claims, exams, evidence, decisions, and receipts.

Lay and witness evidence

VA's current evidence guidance permits lay evidence from the Veteran or people who know about the condition or event. VA currently identifies VA Form 21-10210 as a Lay/Witness Statement form and also lists VA Form 21-4138 as a Statement in Support of Claim.

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE A useful statement identifies what the writer personally observed: what happened, when/where it happened, symptoms they observed, and functional changes. Avoid asking a lay witness to give a medical diagnosis or causation opinion outside their knowledge.

Medical nexus evidence — what makes it useful

For an original direct service-connection claim, VA generally looks for evidence of current disability, an in-service event/injury/disease, and a link between them, unless a presumption or other rule supplies part of the connection.

A medical opinion is stronger when it identifies the records/facts reviewed, states the actual medical conclusion, and explains the reasoning. A conclusory phrase without a rationale can be less persuasive than a reasoned opinion.

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE Give the clinician accurate service and post-service records and ask them to address the actual medical question; do not provide a script that assumes the answer.

Secondary service connection

A secondary claim is not simply “another condition after service.” The evidence should identify the service-connected primary disability, the current secondary disability, and the claimed causal or aggravation relationship.

  • State which service-connected disability is claimed to have caused or aggravated the secondary condition.
  • Submit medical/treatment evidence addressing that relationship when needed.
  • If aggravation is the theory, make that theory explicit rather than describing only causation.

TDIU / Individual Unemployability

VA states that Individual Unemployability can pay at the 100% compensation rate when service-connected disability prevents substantially gainful/steady employment, while the underlying disability rating itself does not necessarily become 100%.

📄 FORMS Current VA guidance uses VA Form 21-8940 and VA Form 21-4192. The 21-4192 is employer employment-information evidence.

  • Document when service-connected conditions began affecting work.
  • Document last full-time work, duties, earnings, accommodations, absences, and why work ended when relevant.
  • Include education and occupational history because employability is not evaluated in a vacuum.
  • If VA later asks you to verify employment while receiving IU, follow the specific notice; VA uses Form 21-4140 in that situation.

C&P exam — report the whole condition accurately

Be honest, specific, and consistent. Describe the severity, frequency, duration, and functional impact of symptoms. Include bad/flaring periods without exaggerating them into the everyday baseline.

  • Review dates, treatment history, medications, and symptom course before the exam.
  • Explain functional effects on work and daily activities where relevant.
  • Do not guess at facts you do not remember.
  • If the examination appears inadequate, identify the specific deficiency: wrong factual premise, missing relevant records, no rationale, failure to address lay evidence, missing required testing, or another concrete problem.

A negative medical opinion does not automatically end the claim

A negative opinion is evidence VA may weigh. Review whether it used an accurate factual premise, considered relevant records and competent lay evidence, gave a reasoned explanation, stayed within examiner competency, and addressed contrary evidence.

A defect does not guarantee another examination or a favorable result. Match the identified defect to the correct decision-review route.

After filing

  1. Watch for VA requests and examination notices.
  2. Upload relevant evidence promptly through the current VA method. VA currently allows additional disability-claim evidence for up to one year after receipt, while warning that it may decide earlier if needed evidence is not supplied promptly.
  3. Save every confirmation and exam notice.
  4. When the decision arrives, read the reasons, favorable findings, effective date, rating, and review-rights notice.
  5. If VA proposes to reduce an existing payment rather than simply deny a new claim, use the separate Benefit Changes procedure.

If VA denies the claim

⚠️ DEADLINE Read the actual decision and review-rights instructions. Use the authorized Supplemental Claim, HLR, or Board Appeal route that fits the case. Open Appeals Navigation.

📋 CHECKLIST

Proof to keep

  • Intent-to-File confirmation, if used
  • Complete 21-526EZ/online claim and supporting forms
  • Medical, service, employment, and lay evidence submitted
  • VA.gov or QuickSubmit confirmation; mailing/fax receipt if used
  • C&P notices and examination reports when available
  • Every VA development letter, decision, and review-rights enclosure
