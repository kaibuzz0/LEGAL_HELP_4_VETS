VA Overpayment & Debt Collection Guide

VA says you owe money? First figure out what kind of debt it is, then pick the right response before the deadline.

Legal Information, Not Legal Advice. This guide explains VA debt processes based on official sources. It does not guarantee a waiver, dispute outcome, or elimination of the debt. Talk to a VA-accredited representative or attorney for help with your specific case.

Deadline Warning

Different VA debt processes have different deadlines. The most important date is the one printed on your VA debt notice or decision letter. Read it carefully. Missing a deadline may limit your options, including your right to a waiver or hearing. [131]

  • Find your notice. The deadline and the type of debt are on the letter or online account.
  • Do not ignore it. Even if you disagree, ignoring a VA debt notice can lead to collection actions, including offsets against future VA benefits or referral to Treasury. [138]
  • If the deadline has passed, ask anyway. You may still have options, but act as soon as possible.

Do This First: 5 Steps

  1. Find the VA debt notice. Look for a letter from VA Debt Management Center or a message in your VA.gov account.
  2. Identify the debt type. Is it a benefit overpayment, education debt, copay bill, home-loan debt, or something else?
  3. Write down dates. Date of the notice, date the debt was created, and any response deadline.
  4. Decide your position. Do you think the debt is wrong, or do you owe it but need relief?
  5. Contact the right office before the deadline. VA debts usually go through the Debt Management Center, but the correct process depends on the debt type.

"I Think the Debt Is Wrong"

If you believe the debt amount, the reason, or the underlying VA decision is incorrect, you generally need to act quickly and in writing. [135]

  1. Get the file. Request a copy of the decision or calculation that created the debt. You have a right to know why VA says you owe money.
  2. Compare with your records. Check award letters, bank deposits, school enrollment records, marriage/dependency records, or medical records depending on the debt type.
  3. Submit a written dispute. Follow the instructions on your debt notice or use VA's online dispute process. Explain exactly what you believe is wrong and include supporting documents.
  4. Keep proof. Save copies of everything you send. Use certified mail or keep electronic confirmation if submitting online.

Important: Disputing the debt itself is different from appealing the underlying VA decision. If the debt was caused by a decision you disagree with — for example, a denial or reduction in disability compensation — you may need to use VA's decision-review process separately. [78]

"I Owe It, But I Cannot Afford It"

If you accept the debt is correct but repayment would cause financial hardship, you may request relief. The main options are waiver, compromise offer, or a payment plan. [133] [134]

Requesting a Waiver

A waiver is a request asking VA to forgive all or part of a debt. Under 38 U.S.C. § 5302, VA may grant a waiver of certain benefit overpayments if recovery would be against equity and good conscience or if specific hardship/fault factors apply. [137]

  • Waiver is generally for benefit overpayments, not every type of VA debt.
  • There is a deadline. Follow the date on your notice or the current VA instructions.
  • You may need to explain your financial situation and why repayment would be unfair or cause hardship.
  • VA does not grant every waiver request. Approval depends on the law and the facts.

Payment Plan / Compromise Offer

  • You can request a monthly payment plan that fits your budget.
  • You may be able to offer a lump-sum compromise for less than the full amount in some cases.
  • Contact the office on your notice or use the online VA debt management tool to set up payments. [130]
"I Disagree With the Underlying VA Decision"

If the debt was created because VA reduced your benefits, denied your claim, or made another decision you disagree with, the debt collection process and the decision review process are usually separate. [78]

  • If you want to challenge the decision: Use VA's decision-review process (Supplemental Claim, Higher-Level Review, or Board Appeal) within the deadline. Winning the underlying decision may eliminate or reduce the debt.
  • If you accept the decision but need debt relief: Use the debt waiver or payment processes described above.
  • You may need to do both. For example, file an appeal on the decision and request a waiver or payment plan on the debt while the appeal is pending.
If VA Is Already Collecting

VA may collect a debt by offsetting future VA benefit payments, referral to the Treasury Offset Program, or other authorized collection actions. [138] [132]

  1. Request a written statement. Ask VA for the exact balance, how much has been collected, and where the remaining debt stands.
  2. Determine if you still have time to dispute or request a waiver. Deadlines may have passed for some options, but others may still be available.
  3. Set up a payment plan if you owe the debt and cannot pay in full. [134]
  4. Contact a VSO or legal-aid attorney if offsets are causing severe hardship. [13]
"Do Not Confuse These"

These are different problems with different procedures. Mixing them up can cost you time and money.

Your situationWhat it usually meansLikely path
"The debt amount or reason is wrong" You disagree that you owe this much or that the debt should exist at all Dispute / review the debt calculation [135]
"The underlying VA decision is wrong" VA reduced or denied a benefit, creating the debt Decision review / appeal [78]
"I owe it, but I can't pay" You accept the debt but repayment would cause hardship Waiver / compromise / payment plan [133] [134]
"I need more time to respond" You need to gather evidence or get help before acting Contact VA Debt Management Center immediately; do not wait until the deadline passes [136]

Sample Letters

Use these as starting points. Replace bracketed text with your information. Keep copies and proof of mailing or electronic delivery.

Sample: Request for Information About a VA Debt

Dear VA Debt Management Center:

I received a debt notice dated [DATE] stating that I owe $[AMOUNT] for [TYPE OF DEBT]. I am requesting a written explanation of how this debt was calculated, including the decision or event that created the debt, the date it was created, and any offset or collection actions taken.

My VA file number / Social Security number is [NUMBER]. Please send the response to [YOUR ADDRESS].

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
[Date]

Sample: Dispute of VA Debt

Dear VA Debt Management Center:

I dispute the debt described in your notice dated [DATE] for $[AMOUNT]. I believe this debt is incorrect because [EXPLAIN REASON, e.g., "I did not receive the benefit amount stated," "my dependency status was correct at the time," "the school certification was wrong"].

Attached are [list supporting documents]. Please review this dispute and provide a written response. [135]

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
[VA File Number]
[Date]

Sample: Request for Waiver of VA Debt

Dear VA Debt Management Center:

I am requesting a waiver of the VA debt described in your notice dated [DATE] for $[AMOUNT]. I understand that a waiver may be considered when recovery would be against equity and good conscience or when repayment would cause financial hardship. [137]

[Explain your financial situation or why repayment would be unfair. Attach supporting documents such as income statements, medical bills, or other debts if applicable.]

Please consider this request and respond in writing. [133]

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
[VA File Number]
[Date]

Documents to Save

Gather these if they apply to your case:

  • The VA debt notice and any follow-up letters
  • The VA decision or award letter that created the debt
  • Bank records or payment stubs showing what you actually received
  • School enrollment records (for education debts)
  • Marriage, divorce, or dependency records (for dependency/indemnity debts)
  • Medical or financial hardship documentation (for waiver requests)
  • Copies of every letter or form you submit
  • Proof of delivery (certified mail receipts, electronic submission confirmations)
Common Terms
Overpayment
VA paid you more than you were entitled to under the law. A debt notice may follow. [131]
Waiver
A request asking VA to forgive all or part of a debt when the law allows. Approval is not guaranteed. [137]
Offset
When VA or another agency reduces your payments to collect a debt. [138]
Treasury Offset Program (TOP)
A Treasury program that intercepts federal and some state payments to collect delinquent federal debts, including some VA debts. [138]
Decision review
The process to challenge a VA benefits decision that caused or affected a debt. [78]

Where to Get Help

  • VA Debt Management Center: Use the contact information on your notice or visit the online tool. [136] [130]
  • VA-accredited VSO or agent: Can help with disputes, waivers, and appeals. [13]
  • Legal aid: Many states have veterans legal clinics. See state-resources.html. [101]
  • VA Crisis Line: If debt stress is becoming a crisis, call or text 988 and press 1, or chat at veteranscrisisline.net. [1]
