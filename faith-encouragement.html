FAITH & ENCOURAGEMENT

This page offers Psalms for veterans and families who draw strength from faith. It is not legal advice, medical advice, or a substitute for crisis care.

What This Is and Who Qualifies

This page offers selected Psalms from the King James Version (KJV) Bible for veterans and families who draw strength from faith. It is not legal advice, medical advice, or a substitute for crisis care. [16]

Who qualifies: Anyone who finds encouragement in scripture. Faith is an optional layer; no one is required to share any belief to receive help from this site. [16]

How to Use This Page in Crisis or Daily Stress

  1. Read the featured verse slowly. You can read it aloud, copy it into a journal, or save it on your phone.
  2. If you are in immediate danger, put the scripture down and call for help: 988 and press 1, or 911. [1]
  3. Faith can be part of recovery, but it works best alongside medical care, legal help, and community support.
  4. Share verses with family or a chaplain if that is helpful; do not use them to pressure someone to avoid care.

All Available Verses

Each time you load a page on this site, one of these verses may appear in the top and bottom banners. The top and bottom banners match on a given page load. Refresh to see another. [16]

  • Psalm 23:1–2 — “The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want. He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters.”
  • Psalm 27:1 — “The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? the LORD is the strength of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?”
  • Psalm 46:1 — “God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.”
  • Psalm 91:1–2 — “He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty. I will say of the LORD, He is my refuge and my fortress: my God; in him will I trust.”
  • Psalm 121:1–2 — “I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help. My help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven and earth.”
  • Psalm 34:17–18 — “The righteous cry, and the LORD heareth, and delivereth them out of all their troubles. The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Crisis Reminder

If you or someone you love is in immediate crisis, faith is a comfort — but professional help is the priority.

  • Veterans Crisis Line: Call or text 988, then press 1 [1]
  • SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357 [2]
  • Emergency: Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.

Chaplains: VA chaplains are available through VA pastoral care for spiritual support that respects your beliefs. [74]

Finding Chaplain and Pastoral Care Support

VA chaplains provide spiritual and pastoral care for veterans, families, and staff regardless of religious affiliation. [74]

  1. Contact your local VA medical center's chaplain service and ask to speak with a chaplain.
  2. Explain what you need: prayer, counseling, grief support, or help finding a faith community in your area.
  3. If you are not enrolled in VA health care, ask whether community chaplain or faith-based resources are available.
Script: "I am a veteran [or family member] looking for chaplain or pastoral care support. Can I speak with a chaplain or get a referral to a faith-based support resource in my area?"

Crisis Reminder

If you are in immediate crisis, faith encouragement is a supplement, not a replacement for emergency care:

  • Veterans Crisis Line: 988, press 1 [1]
  • SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357 [2]
  • Emergency: Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.
