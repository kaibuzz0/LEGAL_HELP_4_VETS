Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA): Know Which Protection Applies

The SCRA protects active-duty servicemembers and, in some sections, their dependents. It does not cover every veteran in every housing situation. The protection depends on the specific problem and the servicemember's duty status.

ProblemSCRA Section / RuleWho QualifiesWhat to Do
Civil lawsuit or default judgment (including eviction case) 50 U.S.C. § 3931 protects against default judgments; § 3932 allows a stay of proceedings if military service prevents appearance. [122] [123] Active-duty servicemembers and, for § 3932, those with notice of the proceeding. Tell the court and opposing party you are on active duty. Request a stay in writing. Get JAG or legal assistance help.
High interest rate on a debt taken out before active duty 50 U.S.C. § 3937 caps interest at 6% per year during covered military service (and, for home mortgages, for one year after; for other obligations, generally six months after service). [124] Servicemember who incurred the debt before entering military service. Dependents may also benefit if the debt was incurred by the servicemember. Send a written notice to the creditor with your active-duty orders. Request the rate reduction and a refund of excess interest.
Eviction from rented housing 50 U.S.C. § 3951 limits eviction when monthly rent is below a statutory cap (adjusted periodically) and requires a court order. [125] Active-duty servicemembers and their dependents in covered rental housing. Contact your installation legal assistance office immediately. Do not rely on SCRA alone to stop an eviction without telling the court.
Foreclosure on a home bought before active duty 50 U.S.C. § 3953 restricts foreclosure and related sale of real property owned before entering military service. [126] Servicemember who owns the property and entered service after obtaining the mortgage. Contact JAG/legal assistance and your VA loan servicer. Request loss mitigation and assert SCRA protections in writing.

Important: "Veteran" status by itself does not trigger SCRA. You generally may need to be in active military service (or recently released, depending on the section). If you are a veteran no longer serving, SCRA may not apply; use VA, SSVF, HUD-VASH, and other veteran-specific programs instead.

Housing Safety: What Not to Do

  • Do not ignore an eviction notice or court summons. A court can enter a default judgment against you if you do not appear or respond, even if you have applied for help. [122] [101]
  • Do not assume that applying for assistance may be available through stops an eviction. You may still need to file an answer in court or ask the court for a continuance while your application is pending. [101]
  • Do not assume veteran status alone creates SCRA protection. SCRA generally applies to active-duty servicemembers and, in some sections, their dependents during covered service. [125]
  • Do not send your complete medical records unless necessary. For a reasonable accommodation, a short provider letter explaining the disability-related need is usually enough. [129]
  • Do not give original documents to anyone except the court or a verified agency. Keep copies of everything you submit.
  • Do not stop making VA mortgage payments without a written agreement. Continue paying what you can while you negotiate loss mitigation. [127]
  • Do not rely on an old phone number without checking it. Agency numbers and programs change. Verify through the official source links on this page.

Reasonable Accommodation Under the Fair Housing Act

The Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f), prohibits housing discrimination based on disability and requires covered housing providers to grant reasonable accommodations when a person with a disability needs a change in rules, practices, or services to use and enjoy the housing. [128] [129]

A request may need to generally be:

  • Made by or for a person with a disability: The disability can be physical or mental. It does not have to be service-connected.
  • Necessary for full use and enjoyment of the housing: There may need to be a disability-related need connecting the accommodation to the housing.
  • Reasonable: It may need to not impose an undue financial or administrative burden on the landlord or fundamentally alter the nature of the housing operation.

What to request: Reserved accessible parking, permission for an assistance animal, a transfer to a ground-floor unit, a different rent-due date tied to disability income, or other disability-related changes.

How to make the request: Put it in writing. Explain that you have a disability and describe the disability-related need. You generally do not need to disclose your complete medical records; a letter from a health-care provider or other reliable source is usually enough. [129]

Response deadline: Federal law does not set a single fixed response deadline for all landlords. To protect yourself, ask for a response within ten business days and keep a copy of your request. If the landlord denies the request or does not respond, you can file a complaint with HUD within one year or a lawsuit within two years in most cases. [129]

If they say no: Ask for the denial in writing with the specific reason. If the reason is "undue burden" or "fundamental alteration," you can offer to pay for the change or suggest an alternative. File a HUD complaint if you believe the denial is discriminatory. [129]

VA-Backed Home Loan: Trouble Making Payments

If you have a VA-potentially available if eligible or VA-held loan and are falling behind, act quickly. Contact your loan servicer first—the company that sends your mortgage statements. Ask for the loss-mitigation or home-retention department. [127]

Possible options include:

  • Repayment plan: Catch up by paying extra each month for a set period.
  • Forbearance: Temporary reduction or pause of payments, usually after a hardship.
  • Loan modification: Permanent change to loan terms to make payments affordable.
  • VA Partial Claim: VA may offer a no-interest second lien to bring the loan current, available in specific programs and subject to eligibility. Verify current availability with your servicer or VA. [127]

What to say: "I am a veteran with a VA-backed loan. I am having trouble making payments because [reason]. I want to apply for loss mitigation before I fall further behind. Please send me the application and tell me the deadline."

If the servicer refuses or you cannot reach them: Contact a VA loan technician through the VA regional loan center that holds your loan guarantee. Keep a call log of every contact. [127]

Do not stop making payments without an agreement. Even if you are waiting for assistance, continue paying what you can and document each payment.
