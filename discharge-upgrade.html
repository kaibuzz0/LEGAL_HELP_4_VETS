Fix Your Discharge or Military Record

Last legally verified: 2026-08-27
Primary/current authority: 10 U.S.C. §§1552(b), 1553; current VA discharge-upgrade instructions; current VA Character of Discharge guidance; official DoD DD Forms 293 and 149.
Branch warning: use the current VA/branch instructions to identify the correct board and submission method.
📋 CHECKLIST

Choose the route before you build the packet

  1. Get the DD-214/separation documents and relevant personnel/medical/disciplinary records.
  2. Identify exactly what you want changed: discharge characterization, narrative reason, reenlistment code, another military-record entry, or VA eligibility only.
  3. Use VA's current discharge-upgrade/correction tool to identify the service board and form.
  4. Check the real filing rule for that route.
  5. Build evidence around the claimed error, injustice, impropriety, inequity, or eligibility issue.
  6. Keep a complete filing copy and proof of submission.

DRB vs BCMR/BCNR vs VA Character of Discharge

RoutePurposeForm / timing
Discharge Review Board (DRB)Reviews qualifying discharge/dismissal characterization under 10 U.S.C. §1553 and may change a discharge within its statutory authority.📄 FORM DD Form 293. ⚠️ DEADLINE §1553 requires a request within 15 years after discharge or dismissal.
BCMR / BCNRBroader correction of military records under 10 U.S.C. §1552, including certain discharge, code, DD-214, and other record issues.📄 FORM DD Form 149. ⚠️ DEADLINE §1552(b) generally uses 3 years after discovery of the error/injustice, with discretionary interest-of-justice waiver.

📚 AUTHORITY

DRB — what the 15-year rule actually says

10 U.S.C. §1553 requires a discharge-review request to be made within 15 years after discharge or dismissal. The statute excludes discharge or dismissal by sentence of a general court-martial from the ordinary DRB review route and limits certain court-martial-related action to clemency-type discharge changes.

If a DRB does not grant an upgrade request, §1553 states that the declination may be considered under §1552 or §1553a, as applicable. That does not mean every later correction-board case will succeed or that the §1552 filing rule disappears.

⚠️ DEADLINE

BCMR/BCNR does not have “no deadline”

Under 10 U.S.C. §1552(b), a correction request generally must be filed within three years after discovering the alleged error or injustice. The board may excuse untimeliness when it finds doing so is in the interest of justice. Treat that as a waiver request that should be supported—not as an automatic exception.

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE In a late case, explain when you discovered the issue and why the board should consider the application despite timing.

DD Form 149 is broader than “discharge older than 15 years”

The current official DoD DD Form 149 is titled Application for Correction of Military Record Under the Provisions of Title 10, U.S. Code, Section 1552. It is a military-record correction form, not merely an “old discharge” form. The correct forum depends on the requested correction, prior board history, service branch, and timing.

VA Character of Discharge review — a separate eligibility path

Current VA guidance says some former service members with other-than-honorable, bad-conduct, or other non-honorable discharges may still qualify for VA benefits depending on VA's Character of Discharge determination. VA's COD decision affects VA eligibility; it does not change the military discharge.

When you apply for VA benefits, VA states that it will review the record when a COD determination is needed. Current VA guidance also allows a person who is not presently applying for benefits to request a COD review through Ask VA or by written request to the Evidence Intake Center.

Do not say a benefit claim “forces” VA to grant benefits. The point is that VA may need to decide whether the service is qualifying for VA purposes.

📋 CHECKLIST

Build the military-board evidence packet

  • All DD-214/DD-215 and separation documents.
  • Official Military Personnel File and relevant service treatment/behavioral-health records.
  • NJP, court-martial, investigative, counseling, evaluation, or command records relevant to the separation.
  • Medical evidence addressing PTSD, TBI, MST, behavioral health, or other conditions when they are relevant to the discharge circumstances.
  • Witness/buddy statements based on personal knowledge.
  • Evidence of rehabilitation, treatment, education, employment, community service, or character where the board may consider post-service conduct.
  • A chronological personal statement explaining the events and the precise correction requested.
  • For BCMR/BCNR: evidence supporting timeliness or an interest-of-justice explanation if the filing is late.

Write the personal statement around the board's job

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE A useful statement is factual, chronological, and specific. It should identify the discharge/correction requested, what happened, why the existing record or discharge is erroneous/unjust/improper/inequitable under the applicable standard, and what evidence supports that position.

If PTSD, TBI, MST, sexual orientation, intimate-partner violence, or another trauma-related issue is relevant, identify the diagnosis/evidence and explain the connection to the conduct or separation rather than merely naming the condition.

Records before filing

  • Use current military/National Archives channels to obtain service personnel and separation records.
  • If the dispute involves facts contained in a court-martial, investigation, medical record, or evaluation, try to obtain that specific record rather than relying only on memory.
  • Do not miss a statutory filing period solely because a records request is still pending. If timing is close, get qualified help on how to preserve the board filing while supplementing the record when allowed.

If a board or VA says no

  • DRB denial: a BCMR/BCNR route may remain under §1552, depending on the issue and case history.
  • BCMR/BCNR denial: read the actual decision for reconsideration requirements and any judicial-review implications. Do not assume that simply refiling the same packet creates a new right to review.
  • VA COD/benefit denial: use the VA review-rights notice and the authorized VA review route; this is not a DRB/BCMR appeal.

🚨 GET A LAWYER Seek a military administrative-law attorney or qualified legal clinic for punitive discharges, court-martial issues, repeated board denials, late §1552 filings, or possible federal-court review.

Proof to keep

  • Complete DD Form 293 or DD Form 149 and every exhibit
  • Board submission confirmation/tracking
  • Every board acknowledgment and case number
  • Any hearing notice and transcript/record if available
  • Final board decision
  • Separate copies of all VA COD/benefit correspondence
