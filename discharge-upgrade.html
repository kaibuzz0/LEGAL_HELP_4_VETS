DISCHARGE UPGRADES AND RECORD CORRECTIONS

Your discharge characterization affects VA benefits, jobs, and reputation. If you received an Other Than Honorable, Bad Conduct, or Dishonorable discharge, you may still have paths to VA care and benefits.

What This Is and Who Qualifies

A discharge upgrade or records correction changes the character of your discharge or fixes errors in your military records. The right board depends on your branch of service, the type of discharge you received, and whether you seek a discharge upgrade or a records correction. [82]

Who qualifies: Veterans with discharges that are Other Than Honorable (OTH), Bad Conduct, Entry-Level Separation, or uncharacterized may apply. You can also apply if your DD-214 contains errors or if you believe your discharge was unjust or based on conditions such as PTSD, TBI, MST, or sexual orientation. [29] [39]

DRB vs BCMR vs VA Character of Discharge Review

BoardWhat It DoesBranchForm / Contact
Discharge Review Board (DRB) [82] Upgrades the character of discharge only; cannot change re-enlistment codes or correct records. Army, Navy/Marine Corps, Air Force, Coast Guard each has its own DRB. DD Form 293. File within 15 years of discharge. [39]
Board for Correction of Military/Naval Records (BCMR/BCNR) [39] Corrects military records, including discharge upgrades, re-enlistment codes, and DD-214 errors. Can upgrade AND correct records. Army (ABCMR), Navy/Marine Corps (BCNR), Air Force (AFBCMR), Coast Guard. DD Form 149. No strict deadline, but file as soon as possible. [39]
VA Character of Discharge (COD) Review [38] Determines whether VA can provide benefits despite a less-than-honorable discharge. Does not change the discharge itself. All branches. File a VA claim; VA will conduct a COD review automatically.

Key rule: Even if a discharge upgrade is denied, a VA claim can still force a COD review that may open access to VA health care and compensation. [38] [84]

Step-by-Step: How to Apply for a Discharge Upgrade

  1. Use the VA wizard first. Go to the VA discharge upgrade instructions page and answer the questions. It will tell you whether to use DD Form 293 (DRB) or DD Form 149 (BCMR/BCNR) and which branch address to use. [82]
  2. Get your service records. Request your DD-214, service treatment records, and personnel file from the National Archives at vetrecs.archives.gov or by SF-180. [39]
  3. Gather supporting evidence. Medical records, mental health diagnoses (PTSD/TBI), statements from family or fellow service members, court martial records, and any evidence the discharge was connected to an in-service condition. [29]
  4. Write a personal statement. Explain what happened, why it was connected to service or an in-service condition, and what you have done since discharge. Be specific about dates, units, and witnesses.
  5. Complete the right form:
    • DD Form 293 (DRB): For discharge upgrades within 15 years of separation. [39]
    • DD Form 149 (BCMR/BCNR): For records corrections and upgrades older than 15 years or when you need to change re-enlistment codes. [39]
  6. Mail the package to the correct board. Use certified mail with return receipt requested. Addresses change; confirm the current address on the board’s official website:
    • Army ABCMR / Army DRB: see https://arba.army.mil/ [86]
    • Navy/Marine Corps BCNR / NDRB: see https://www.secnav.navy.mil/mra/bcnr/ [85]
    • Air Force BCMR / DRB: see https://www.afbcmr.af.mil/ [87]
    • Coast Guard BCMR: see DHS Board for Correction of Military Records [88]
  7. File a VA claim anyway. Even if your discharge upgrade is pending, file a VA claim to trigger a Character of Discharge review. This may unlock benefits faster than the board process. [38] [17]
  8. Track deadlines. DRB applications must generally be filed within 15 years of discharge. BCMR/BCNR has no fixed deadline, but delay weakens a case.

Evidence Checklist

  • DD-214 (all copies) and any separation documents
  • Service treatment records and personnel file
  • Post-service medical and mental health records (VA or private)
  • Buddy statements describing the event and your character in service
  • Family statements describing behavior changes after service
  • Court-martial or NJP records, if any
  • Evidence of PTSD/TBI/MST diagnosis or treatment
  • Personal statement with timeline

Sample Personal Statement Opening

“I respectfully request that the [Army Discharge Review Board / Board for Correction of Military Records] upgrade my discharge from [OTH/BCD] to [Honorable/General Under Honorable Conditions]. My discharge on [date] was the direct result of [undiagnosed PTSD / a traumatic brain injury / military sexual trauma / a behavioral health crisis] that began during my service in [unit/location] from [dates]. I am attaching [medical records / buddy statements / family statements] showing that my conduct was not willful misconduct but connected to an in-service condition. Since discharge I have [completed treatment / maintained employment / had no further disciplinary issues]. I respectfully ask the Board to grant the upgrade.”

What If They Say No

  • DRB denied: You can appeal to the BCMR/BCNR of your branch with new evidence or legal argument. [39]
  • BCMR/BCNR denied: You can reapply with new evidence. You can also petition your U.S. Representative or Senators for assistance, though Congress cannot overturn a board decision. [41]
  • VA claim denied due to discharge: Request a Character of Discharge review and submit evidence that your service was “honorable” for VA purposes. [38] [84]

PTSD/TBI/Mental-Health Upgrade Path

If your discharge was related to undiagnosed PTSD, TBI, or another mental health condition, DoD guidance (Hagel, Carter, and Kurta memoranda) may support a more favorable characterization.

  1. Get a current mental health diagnosis from a VA or private provider.
  2. Obtain a nexus opinion linking the in-service trauma to the current diagnosis and to the behavior that led to the discharge.
  3. File with the appropriate board (DRB if within 15 years of discharge; BCNR if more than 15 years or correcting records).
  4. State clearly: "The discharge was the result of symptoms of undiagnosed PTSD/TBI. Current diagnosis and treatment records are attached."

Important: These are not guaranteed upgrades. Each board decides based on the individual record. Do not promise outcomes.

Step-by-Step Upgrade Application

  1. Use the VA discharge upgrade wizard to identify the correct board and form. [29]
  2. Request your complete military personnel file and discharge packet from NPRC or your service's records center.
  3. Write a personal statement explaining what happened, why it happened, how you have changed, and why an upgrade is justified.
  4. Gather supporting documents: post-service treatment records, employment records, character references, and any evidence of PTSD/TBI.
  5. Submit the application to the correct board by mail or online portal, depending on service branch.
  6. Track the case and respond promptly to requests for additional information.

Document Checklist

  • DD-214 and any discharge paperwork
  • Complete service personnel and medical records
  • Post-service medical and mental health records
  • Employment and education records showing rehabilitation
  • Character reference letters from employers, clergy, family, or veterans
  • Nexus opinion linking mental health condition to discharge if applicable

What to Do While Waiting: File a VA Claim Anyway

Even with a bad discharge, VA must conduct a Character of Discharge review if you file a claim for benefits. [38] This is separate from a discharge upgrade and may allow you to receive VA health care and disability compensation while the upgrade application is pending.

  1. File VA Form 21-526EZ for the conditions you believe are service-connected.
  2. VA will issue a decision on whether your discharge is a statutory or regulatory bar.
  3. If the bar is found insufficient for the benefit sought, VA will adjudicate the claim on the merits.
  4. Work with a VSO or attorney experienced in COD cases.

Timeline Expectations and Records to Keep

  • DRB: typically several months to over a year.
  • BCNR: often 1-2 years or longer due to backlog.
  • VA COD review: usually months, varies by regional office.
  • Keep copies of every submission, certified mail receipts, and board correspondence.
