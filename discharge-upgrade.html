Fix Your Discharge: Upgrade, Correct Records, or Request VA Review

DO THIS FIRST

Last legally verified: 2026-08-27
Primary authorities checked: 10 U.S.C. § 1552(b) · current VA discharge-upgrade instructions · current VA Character of Discharge guidance
Forms checked: DD Form 293 · DD Form 149
Branch warning: Service-board procedures and submission addresses vary. Use the current official instructions for your branch.

Choose the right route

  1. Get your DD-214 and personnel/medical records.
  2. Decide whether you need a discharge review, a broader military-record correction, a VA eligibility determination, or more than one route.
  3. Check the filing rule for the board you are using.
  4. Use the current prescribed form.
  5. Submit supporting evidence and keep proof of filing.

DRB vs. BCMR/BCNR vs. VA Character of Discharge

RouteWhat it doesTypical form / timing
Discharge Review Board (DRB)Reviews the propriety/equity of qualifying discharges and can change characterization within its authority.📄 FORM DD Form 293. ⚠️ DEADLINE Generally subject to a 15-year filing period. [39]
BCMR / BCNRCan correct military records and may address discharge characterization, reenlistment codes, DD-214 errors, and other record corrections within its authority.📄 FORM Usually DD Form 149. ⚠️ DEADLINE 10 U.S.C. § 1552(b) generally requires filing within 3 years after discovery of the error or injustice; the board may excuse untimeliness if it finds doing so is in the interest of justice.
VA Character of Discharge reviewVA determines whether a period of service qualifies for VA benefits/services under VA law. It does not change the military discharge itself.VA may make this determination when needed in connection with a benefits application. [38]
⚠️ DEADLINE

BCMR/BCNR does have a statutory filing rule

Do not rely on advice that a BCMR or BCNR application has “no deadline” or “no strict deadline.” Under 10 U.S.C. § 1552(b), a correction request is generally filed within three years after discovering the alleged error or injustice. A board may excuse a late filing when it finds that doing so is in the interest of justice. That waiver is discretionary, not automatic.

📚 AUTHORITY 10 U.S.C. § 1552(b).

DD Form 149 is not only for discharges older than 15 years

📄 FORM DD Form 149 is generally used to apply to a Board for Correction of Military or Naval Records for military-record corrections. That can include matters other than discharge characterization and can include cases where a DRB route is unavailable or insufficient. The correct forum depends on the relief requested, prior board history, branch, and timing.

VA benefits may still be possible without a discharge upgrade

⚖️ YOUR RIGHT A less-than-honorable discharge does not automatically answer every VA-benefit eligibility question. VA may conduct a Character of Discharge determination for VA purposes. VA states that some former service members with undesirable, bad conduct, or other-than-honorable discharges may still qualify depending on VA’s determination. [38]

This VA determination does not change the Armed Forces’ discharge characterization.

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE If you need VA benefits now, do not assume you must wait for a military board to finish before asking VA to determine eligibility. Apply for the benefit and provide the discharge-related evidence VA requests.

📋 CHECKLIST

Evidence to build the case

  • DD-214 and separation packet
  • Official military personnel file
  • Service treatment and behavioral-health records
  • NJP, court-martial, or investigative records if relevant
  • Medical evidence relating PTSD, TBI, MST, behavioral health, or other conditions to the circumstances of discharge when applicable
  • Statements from witnesses, supervisors, family, or treatment providers
  • Post-service rehabilitation, employment, education, treatment, or character evidence when relevant
  • A clear timeline explaining when you discovered the claimed error or injustice

If they say no

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE Read the board decision and identify the exact reason for denial before choosing the next step. A DRB denial may leave a BCMR/BCNR route; a correction-board denial may have reconsideration or judicial-review issues depending on the case; a VA eligibility denial uses VA review procedures rather than military-board procedures.

🚨 GET A LAWYER Consider a military administrative-law attorney or qualified veterans-law clinic for punitive discharges, court-martial records, repeated board denials, statutory timeliness disputes, or possible federal-court review.

YOUR NEXT ACTION: Get your DD-214, use the current VA discharge-upgrade instructions to identify the correct board, and calendar the real filing rule before preparing DD Form 293 or DD Form 149.
