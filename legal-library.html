WORDS MATTER — LEGAL LANGUAGE TRANSLATOR

VA and court systems use words that sound simple but have specific legal meanings. Misunderstanding a term can cost you benefits or delay a case for years. Here is what the words actually mean and how to use them.

Key Terms and How to Use Them

  • Service-connected: A disability VA has linked to military service. Use in claims and appeals to argue for compensation.
  • Nexus: The medical or legal link between service and a current condition. Use to win claims where the connection is not obvious.
  • Effective date: The date VA uses to start paying benefits. Earlier effective dates mean more back pay. Protect it with intent-to-file and timely appeals.
  • C&P exam: Compensation and Pension exam. Use it to support your rating; challenge it if it is incomplete or wrong.
  • Presumptive: VA presumes a condition is service-connected if you served in a specific place/time. Use for Agent Orange, burn pits, and PACT Act conditions. [27]
  • TDIU: Total Disability based on Individual Unemployability. Use when you cannot work due to service-connected disabilities. [26]

Sample Legal Demand Letters

SCRA interest rate reduction request: [35]

"I am writing to request the 6% interest rate cap on my account [number] under the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act, 50 U.S.C. 3937. I entered active duty on [date] and request that interest charged during my active-duty period be reduced to 6% per year, with any excess interest already paid credited to the account. Enclosed is a copy of my active-duty orders. Please respond within 30 days."

Debt validation request:

"I dispute the debt referenced in your [letter/call] dated [date] and request validation under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act. Please provide: the original creditor, the amount owed with itemization, proof I owe the debt, and the authority to collect. Until validation is provided, cease all collection activity."

Reasonable accommodation request (housing): [36]

"I am a tenant at [address] and have a disability-related need for [specific accommodation, e.g., a reserved accessible parking space, permission for an emotional support animal, a transfer to a ground-floor unit]. Under the Fair Housing Act, I request this reasonable accommodation. I have attached [medical/provider verification if required]. Please respond within 10 business days."

LEGAL REFERENCE LIBRARY

These are the statutes, regulations, and programs behind the claims in this manual. Each entry includes a practical note on how to use it.

Federal Statutes and Regulations

  • 38 U.S.C. (Veterans' Benefits): The main federal law for VA benefits. Use it to argue entitlement to compensation, pension, education, and housing benefits.
  • 38 C.F.R. Part 3: VA regulations on adjudication of claims. Key sections include 3.159 (duty to assist), 3.303 (service connection), 3.307/3.309 (presumptions), and 4.3 (evaluation of disabilities). [37] [42]
  • 50 U.S.C. Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA): Protects active-duty servicemembers from default judgments, interest rate spikes, lease penalties, and foreclosure. Use for financial and housing emergencies. [35]
  • Fair Housing Act: Prohibits disability discrimination in housing. Use for reasonable accommodation requests and discrimination complaints. [36]
  • PACT Act: Adds presumptive conditions for toxic exposure. Use to claim conditions without needing a nexus letter if you served in a covered location. [27]

How to Use a Citation in a Letter or Appeal

"Under 38 C.F.R. 3.159, VA has a duty to assist the claimant in obtaining relevant records. VA did not obtain the service treatment records from [facility] that I identified in my claim. Without those records, VA cannot properly evaluate my claim for [condition]."
"The PACT Act, Pub. L. No. 117-168, added [condition] as a presumptive condition for veterans who served in [covered location]. As shown on my DD-214, I served in [location] during [dates]. Therefore, service connection is presumed and no separate medical nexus is required."
YOUR NEXT ACTION: If you are appealing a denial, identify the regulation VA cited against you and find the argument that turns it in your favor. A VSO or attorney can help.
