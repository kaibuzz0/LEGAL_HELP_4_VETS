I NEED HELP NOW

If You Are Sleeping in Your Truck Tonight

Do not wait. Do not try to solve everything at once. Start with one phone call.

Call 1-877-4AID-VET (1-877-424-3838)

  • Say: "I am a veteran. I am homeless / about to be evicted / sleeping in my vehicle. I need help now."
  • Give your location.
  • Ask for: SSVF emergency help, HUD-VASH referral, local outreach worker.

Gather these documents today:

  • DD-214
  • Photo ID
  • Lease, eviction notice, or utility shutoff notice
  • Proof of income or unemployment
  • IDs for spouse/children if they are with you

Also call:

  • 211 for local shelter, food, utility, and housing resources.
  • Your local VA medical center emergency department for urgent medical needs.

If You Are in Medical Crisis

Call 988 and press 1 for the Veterans Crisis Line. Text 988 or chat at 988lifeline.org if you cannot speak safely.

If you need urgent care but it is not life-threatening:

  • Call your VA medical center's nurse advice line.
  • Go to a VA urgent care clinic if enrolled.
  • Go to the nearest emergency room if VA is not an option. You can seek VA reimbursement later.

Document everything:

  • Who you spoke to.
  • Date, time, and what they said.
  • If care is denied, ask for the denial in writing.
  • File a complaint with the VA Patient Advocate if needed.

If You Need Food or Money Immediately

  • SNAP (food stamps): Apply through your state social services agency. Veterans are often eligible.
  • Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF): State-administered cash assistance.
  • Unemployment: File with your state unemployment office.
  • VA pension: If wartime veteran with limited income.
  • SSDI/SSI: If your disabilities prevent work.
  • SSVF: May cover rent, utilities, deposits, moving costs.

KNOW YOUR STATUS

Service Type

StatusWhat It Means
Active dutyFull-time service. Most federal veteran benefits require you to have completed the service obligation and received a qualifying discharge.
National GuardState-controlled unless federalized. Federal VA benefits usually require federal active service. State benefits may still apply.
ReservePart-time unless activated. Benefits depend on active-duty periods, activation orders, and character of discharge.
Combat veteranMay qualify for enhanced VA health care, presumptions, and expedited claims.
Recently separatedYou may still have SCRA protections, transitional health care, and deadlines that are running now.

Discharge Type

CharacterizationEffect on VA Benefits
HonorableFull eligibility for VA benefits.
General (Under Honorable Conditions)Usually eligible for most VA benefits.
Other Than HonorableMay be barred, but VA can still make a Character of Discharge determination. File anyway.
Bad ConductOften barred. Apply for upgrade and for VA COD determination.
DishonorableGenerally barred. Seek upgrade and legal help.

Health and Disability

SituationPossible Path
Injury or illness during serviceFile a VA disability compensation claim.
Condition existed before service but got worseFile for aggravation of pre-existing condition.
Condition caused by a service-connected disabilityFile for secondary service connection.
Cannot work due to service-connected disabilitiesFile for Total Disability based on Individual Unemployability (TDIU).
Needs help with daily livingLook into VA Aid and Attendance or caregiver support.

Exposure History

ExposurePossible Benefit
Agent OrangePresumptive conditions for Vietnam, Korea DMZ, and certain other locations.
Burn pits / airborne hazardsPACT Act creates presumptions for many respiratory and cancers.
Gulf War exposuresPresumptive chronic multisymptom illness.
Camp Lejeune waterVA health care and compensation for covered conditions.
Radiation, asbestos, other toxic exposuresSpecific presumptions and claims possible.

Route Yourself

If you are recently separated, homeless, and have an Other Than Honorable discharge:

  • Call 1-877-4AID-VET for housing help.
  • File a VA claim anyway to force a Character of Discharge determination.
  • Apply for a discharge upgrade.

If you are combat veteran with undiagnosed health problems:

  • Enroll in VA health care as a combat veteran.
  • Get screened for PACT Act conditions.
  • File for presumptive service connection if a condition matches.

If you cannot work:

  • File for VA disability if not already service-connected.
  • File for TDIU if already service-connected.
  • Apply for SSDI through Social Security.

CLAIMS BATTLE ROOM

The One Rule That Wins Claims

The VA does not know your story unless you build the record.

Your job is to make the connection between:

  1. Something that happened in service, and
  2. A current diagnosed condition, and
  3. A current level of disability.

If any of those three is weak, your claim is weak.

Evidence Checklist

Use this before filing any claim.

Service Records

  • DD-214
  • Service treatment records
  • Personnel file
  • Deployment orders
  • Awards and decorations
  • Unit records or after-action reports

Medical Records

  • VA treatment records
  • Private doctor records
  • Emergency room visits
  • Specialist opinions
  • Imaging, lab results, test reports

Personal Statements

  • Your lay statement (VA Form 21-4138)
  • Spouse or family member statement
  • Buddy statements from fellow service members
  • Employer or coworker statement about work limitations

Exposure and Event Documentation

  • Deployment locations and dates
  • Unit newsletters or records showing exposures
  • Photos, maps, incident reports
  • Newspaper articles or government reports about the event

Building the Nexus

The nexus is the medical link between service and your current condition. The VA standard is:

"At least as likely as not" the condition was caused or aggravated by service.

Get a medical opinion in that exact language.

Good nexus language:

"After reviewing the veteran's service treatment records and post-service treatment records, it is my opinion that it is at least as likely as not that [current condition] was caused by / aggravated by / secondary to the veteran's military service / service-connected [condition]."

Weak nexus language to avoid:

  • "Could be related."
  • "Might be from service."
  • "Possibly connected."

Filing the Claim

  1. Go to VA.gov or use a VSO.
  2. File VA Form 21-526EZ for initial claims.
  3. Keep a copy of everything submitted.
  4. Get confirmation of filing.
  5. Track your claim online or by calling 1-800-827-1000.

Protecting Your Effective Date

If a deadline is approaching and your evidence is not ready:

  • File a protective claim before the deadline.
  • Submit the full evidence as soon as possible.
  • The effective date may be worth thousands of dollars in back pay.

WORDS MATTER — LEGAL LANGUAGE TRANSLATOR

Weak vs. Strong Statements

WeakStrong
My back hurts.I experience chronic lumbar pain with functional impairment affecting standing, walking, lifting, sleep, and employment activities.
I think this happened because of service.The condition began during active service / was aggravated by service / is secondary to my service-connected [condition].
I can't work.My service-connected disabilities prevent me from securing or following a substantially gainful occupation.
I have trouble sleeping.I experience chronic sleep disturbance with daytime fatigue, concentration deficits, and functional impairment.
The doctor didn't listen.The C&P examination failed to review relevant records, address the claimed symptoms, or provide an adequate medical opinion for rating purposes.
I was treated unfairly.The denial appears inconsistent with the evidence of record and the applicable rating criteria.

Sample Statements by Situation

Lay Statement (VA Form 21-4138)

In [month/year], while stationed at [location] and serving with [unit], I [describe in-service event or exposure]. Since that time, I have experienced [specific symptoms]. These symptoms occur approximately [frequency] and limit my ability to [specific activities]. On my worst days, [describe worst day]. This statement is made from my personal knowledge and is true.

Requesting Accommodation

I am a tenant/resident/employee and a veteran with a service-connected disability. I request a reasonable accommodation under the Fair Housing Act / Americans with Disabilities Act: [specific accommodation]. This accommodation is necessary because of my disability. Attached is supporting documentation.

SCRA Interest Rate Reduction

Pursuant to the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act, 50 U.S.C. § 3937, I entered active duty on [date], and this obligation predates that date. I request that you reduce the interest rate to 6% per annum from [date] and refund or credit all interest charged above 6%. A copy of my orders is attached.

Debt Validation

Pursuant to the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1692g, I dispute this debt and request complete validation, including the name of the original creditor, original account number, complete payment history, and proof you are authorized to collect this debt. Until validation is provided, cease all collection activity.

APPEALS NAVIGATION

Denied? Do Not Stop.

A VA denial is not the end. It is the beginning of the next phase. You have options.

Three Lanes After a Decision

LaneWhen to UseWhat It DoesDeadline
Supplemental ClaimYou have new and relevant evidence.Reopens the issue with new evidence.1 year from decision for effective date protection.
Higher-Level ReviewYou believe VA made a mistake and you do not have new evidence.A senior reviewer re-examines the same record.1 year.
Board AppealYou want a Veterans Law Judge to review your case.Full appellate review. Evidence can be added at certain stages.1 year.

Appeals Strategy

  1. Read the rating decision's Reasons and Bases section.
  2. Identify exactly why VA denied or rated lower than expected.
  3. Choose the lane that matches your situation.
  4. File before the deadline.
  5. Request a copy of your C-file (complete claims file) with VA Form 20-10206.

Common Appeal Arguments

Inadequate C&P Exam

The exam was too short, ignored records, used an unqualified examiner, or contradicted medical evidence. Request a new exam or submit an independent medical opinion.

Benefit of the Doubt

Under 38 C.F.R. § 3.102, when the evidence is roughly equal, the veteran wins. Argue this when the record supports your claim but VA chose against it.

Staged Ratings

If your condition got worse over time, you may be entitled to separate ratings for separate time periods. This can increase back pay.

Secondary Conditions

A service-connected condition caused or worsened another condition. File separately for each secondary condition with a nexus opinion.

Duty to Assist

VA must help get relevant records. If VA failed to obtain records, request a remand or supplemental development.

Appeals Deadlines

  • 1 year from the date of the rating decision to appeal or file a supplemental claim for effective date protection.
  • Missing the deadline does not always end your case, but it may cost you months or years of back pay.

THINGS VETERANS OFTEN DON'T KNOW

Benefits and Protections That Get Overlooked

Secondary Conditions

A service-connected condition can cause other problems. Examples:

  • Service-connected knee injury causing back problems.
  • PTSD causing sleep apnea or GERD.
  • Medication side effects causing new conditions.

Increased Ratings

If a service-connected condition worsens, file for an increased rating. Do not wait for VA to notice.

Presumptive Conditions

If you served in certain places or times, VA presumes certain conditions are service-connected. The PACT Act added many new presumptions for burn pits and toxic exposures.

Unemployability Benefits (TDIU)

Even if your combined rating is not 100%, you may be paid at the 100% rate if your service-connected disabilities prevent substantially gainful employment.

Caregiver Support

If a veteran needs help with daily activities because of service-connected disabilities, the Primary Family Caregiver program may provide financial support, training, and respite care.

State Veteran Benefits

Many states offer property tax exemptions, education benefits, employment preferences, hunting/fishing licenses, and more. Check your state veterans affairs department.

Education Benefits

  • Post-9/11 GI Bill
  • Montgomery GI Bill
  • Vocational Rehabilitation and Employment (Chapter 31)
  • State tuition assistance

Burial and Memorial Benefits

Eligible veterans may receive burial in a national cemetery, a gravesite flag, a Presidential Memorial Certificate, and a headstone or marker.

VA Home Loan

VA-backed loans often require no down payment and no private mortgage insurance. Available to many veterans with qualifying service.

SGLI / VGLI Conversion

Service members' Group Life Insurance can convert to Veterans' Group Life Insurance after separation. Know your conversion deadlines.

FINAL WORDS

The system is slow. The system is complicated. The system sometimes says no when it should say yes.

But veterans have won claims, kept homes, stopped wrongful evictions, defeated bad debt collection, upgraded discharges, and recovered benefits after years of denial.

The difference is usually not luck. It is preparation, persistence, paperwork, and the right words at the right time.

This manual is a starting point. Use it. Share it. Update it. Add your own notes. And when you can, help the next veteran behind you.

You served. You earned the fight.

FOREIGN CITIZENSHIP & MILITARY IMMIGRATION

Many service members and veterans are not U.S. citizens. Citizenship status affects your military service, your VA benefits, your family’s ability to immigrate, and your risk of deportation. This section covers the most common issues and the agencies involved.

Quick Eligibility Rules

TopicRule in Plain Language
Serving while not a citizenLawful permanent residents (green-card holders) and certain other non-citizens can enlist. Some positions and security clearances require U.S. citizenship.
Military naturalizationUnder INA § 328, lawful permanent residents who serve honorably during peacetime or hostilities may be eligible for expedited naturalization.
Naturalization during hostilitiesUnder INA § 329, certain service members who served during designated periods of hostilities can naturalize without first becoming permanent residents.
VA benefits for non-citizensMost VA benefits require a qualifying discharge and may require U.S. citizenship or lawful permanent resident status. Some benefits are limited by immigration status.
Deportation riskA criminal conviction or unfavorable discharge can make a non-citizen veteran removable. Get immigration counsel before any plea or discharge upgrade.

How to Apply for Military Naturalization

  1. Confirm your immigration status is current (lawful permanent resident, parolee, or otherwise eligible category).
  2. Request certified copies of your military service records (DD-214, service-characterization documents).
  3. Complete USCIS Form N-400, Application for Naturalization, or N-426 for military filing.
  4. Submit through the correct USCIS channel — many military applicants use a designated naturalization office or file with help from a JAG paralegal.
  5. Track your case online at myaccount.uscis.dhs.gov.
  6. Respond immediately to any Request for Evidence or notice of intent to deny.

YOUR NEXT ACTION: If you are still serving, ask your installation’s legal assistance office or JAG for help with the N-400/N-426 package.

Benefits and Limits for Non-Citizen Veterans

BenefitCitizenship / Immigration Requirement
VA disability compensationUsually available to honorably discharged veterans regardless of citizenship, but some non-citizens may face restrictions depending on service location and characterization.
VA health careGenerally available to veterans with qualifying discharge. Immigration status may affect enrollment for some categories.
VA pensionRequires wartime service, limited income, and may require citizenship or legal permanent resident status.
VA education (GI Bill)Usually requires an honorable discharge. Citizenship is not always required, but residency and discharge rules apply.
VA home loanCitizenship is not required, but you must meet service, credit, and occupancy requirements.
Burial benefitsDischarge and service requirements apply; citizenship is not usually the deciding factor.

Family Immigration Through Military Service

  • Parole in place: Certain undocumented family members of U.S. citizen service members, veterans, and reservists may request “parole in place” to remain in the United States while pursuing a green card.
  • Survivor benefits: Family members of service members who died may be eligible for immigration benefits; timing and filing location matter.
  • Military deployment and domicile: Some family-based immigration rules depend on where the sponsor is “domiciled.” Military service can complicate this — use a base address only when the regulation allows.
  • Overseas spouses and children: Filing at a U.S. consulate abroad versus adjusting status inside the U.S. changes processing time and risk. Get legal help before choosing.

Discharge Upgrades and Deportation Defense

  • A bad-conduct or other-than-honorable discharge can cost you both VA benefits and immigration status.
  • Veterans facing removal may be able to apply for citizenship, a discharge upgrade, or post-conviction relief to remove the deportation trigger.
  • Do not wait. Immigration court deadlines are short, and a missed deadline can end your case.
  • Ask an accredited representative or immigration attorney whether a Character of Discharge determination, clemency, or veteran-specific immigration relief applies to you.
