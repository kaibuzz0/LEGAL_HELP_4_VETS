VA Medical Rights

Last legally verified: 2026-08-27
Primary authorities: VA emergency-care guidance; 38 CFR §§ 17.4000–17.4040; VA Patient Advocate guidance; VA Privacy Act guidance; 38 U.S.C. §1151; 38 CFR §3.361; 28 U.S.C. §§2401(b), 2675; VA OGC FTCA instructions.
Forms checked: SF 95.
State-law warning: FTCA liability can depend on the law of the place where the act or omission occurred.
⚖️ YOUR RIGHT

Medical emergency now

If you think your life or health is in danger, call 911 or go to the nearest appropriate emergency department. You do not need to wait for VA authorization before seeking emergency treatment.

⚠️ DEADLINE For non-VA emergency care, VA currently asks to receive notification within 72 hours after emergency care starts. The provider is preferred, but the Veteran or someone acting on the Veteran's behalf can notify VA. Late notice is not an automatic denial: VA states the claim may then be considered under unauthorized-emergency-care rules.

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE Ask the emergency department to notify VA promptly and save proof of the visit, discharge papers, bills, and any VA notification confirmation.

📚 AUTHORITY VA: Getting emergency care at non-VA facilities.

VA denied or delayed care

  1. Immediate safety first: if the delay has become an emergency, use the emergency route above.
  2. Identify the decision: was this a clinical judgment, scheduling/access issue, Community Care eligibility decision, payment issue, medication decision, or administrative denial?
  3. Save: referral, appointment messages, portal messages, denial language, dates, names, and relevant medical records.
  4. Start local: ask the treatment team or responsible service to explain what was decided and why.
  5. Patient Advocate: if unresolved, contact the VA medical center Patient Advocate. VA describes the program as a route for Veterans and families to address care concerns and patient rights.
  6. Clinical dispute: ask whether the VHA clinical appeals process applies. Do not assume every scheduling, billing, or eligibility issue is a clinical appeal.
  7. Community Care: if the dispute is about Community Care, ask which specific eligibility criterion under 38 CFR §17.4010 was evaluated and whether authorization was required.

📚 AUTHORITY VA Patient Advocate Program.

Community Care decision tree

  1. Do you need emergency care? If yes, use the emergency-care rules, not the ordinary referral pathway.
  2. Did VA authorize or refer the care? Ordinary Community Care generally requires VA authorization before treatment.
  3. Does an eligibility criterion apply? Under 38 CFR §17.4010, examples include a needed service not offered by VA, inability to furnish care within designated access standards, certain grandfathered circumstances, best-medical-interest determinations, and quality-standard issues.
  4. Was the problem wait time or drive time? Ask for the individualized eligibility determination. VA's published average wait-time website does not itself determine Community Care eligibility.
  5. Did VA deny payment after non-VA care? Separate that from eligibility for prospective Community Care. Unauthorized emergency-care payment rules can be legally different from the Community Care Program.

📚 AUTHORITY 38 CFR §17.4010.

Mental-health emergency

If there is immediate danger of self-harm or another medical emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest appropriate emergency department. Veterans may also call or text 988 and press 1 for the Veterans Crisis Line. Do not delay emergency mental-health care while trying to obtain routine authorization.

Get and correct VA medical records

  1. GET THE RECORD: request the relevant VA medical record through the appropriate VA records/Privacy Act process.
  2. IDENTIFY THE ERROR: specify the exact entry, date, author, and why you believe it is inaccurate, incomplete, untimely, or irrelevant.
  3. REQUEST AMENDMENT: use VA's Privacy Act amendment process where applicable.
  4. SAVE THE RESPONSE: keep the request, proof of submission, and VA's written response.
  5. ESCALATE IF DENIED: follow the review/appeal instructions in the response rather than inventing a local escalation route.

📚 AUTHORITY VA states that Privacy Act requests can be used to access one's records and seek amendment of records maintained by VA that are inaccurate, incomplete, untimely, or irrelevant. VA Privacy Act Requests.

C-file is different: a VA disability claims file is not the same thing as ordinary VHA medical records. Use the claims-record process when you need the C-file.

⚖️ YOUR RIGHT

VA caused harm: 38 U.S.C. §1151 benefits route

Section 1151 is a VA benefits route, not a malpractice lawsuit. For modern claims, 38 CFR §3.361 requires analysis of additional disability or death, actual causation by qualifying VA care/treatment/examination, and proximate cause. Depending on the facts, proximate cause may involve VA fault such as carelessness, negligence, lack of proper skill, error in judgment, or a similar instance of fault, or an event not reasonably foreseeable; informed-consent rules can also matter.

A bad outcome by itself does not establish §1151 entitlement, and this page does not decide whether a particular treatment met the legal standard.

📋 CHECKLIST Preserve pre-treatment records, consent documents, procedure notes, discharge instructions, post-treatment records, independent medical opinions, and a timeline of the claimed additional disability.

📚 AUTHORITY 38 U.S.C. §1151 · 38 CFR §3.361.

🚨 GET A LAWYER

Federal Tort Claims Act (FTCA)

FTCA is a separate tort claim against the United States. VA OGC states that it can provide money compensation for qualifying injury caused by a wrongful act or omission of a federal employee acting within the scope of federal employment, and that it does not govern acts of non-VA medical facilities, community-care providers, or independent contractors.

⚠️ DEADLINE 28 U.S.C. §2401(b) generally requires a tort claim to be presented in writing to the appropriate federal agency within 2 years after the claim accrues, and an action to be begun within 6 months after the agency mails final denial. Accrual can be legally complex—do not wait for the deadline to get advice.

📄 FORM VA says SF 95 is a convenient way to present the claim but is not mandatory if the written presentment otherwise satisfies VA's requirements. VA and DOJ both emphasize the need for a sum certain—a specific total dollar amount claimed.

Administrative exhaustion: under 28 U.S.C. §2675(a), an FTCA lawsuit generally cannot be instituted until the claim has first been presented to the agency and finally denied in writing, or the agency has failed to make final disposition within six months and the claimant elects to treat that as a denial for purposes of suit.

📚 AUTHORITY VA OGC FTCA instructions · 28 U.S.C. §2401 · 28 U.S.C. §2675.

If they say no

  1. Save the denial or clinical decision.
  2. Record the date received.
  3. Identify whether the issue is clinical judgment, eligibility, authorization, payment, records, benefits, or tort liability.
  4. Use only the review route that actually governs that category.
  5. Preserve proof and the medical record.
  6. Get representation early for §1151/FTCA or serious care-access disputes.
