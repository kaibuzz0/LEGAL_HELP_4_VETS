VA Medical Rights

Last legally verified: 2026-08-27
Primary authorities/guidance: VA emergency-care guidance; 38 CFR §17.4010; VA Clinical Appeals; VHA health-benefit decision review; VA Patient Advocate; PCAFC decision-review guidance; VA Privacy Act; 38 U.S.C. §1151; 38 CFR §3.361; 28 U.S.C. §§2401(b), 2675; VA OGC FTCA instructions.
State-law warning: FTCA liability can depend on the law of the place where the act or omission occurred.
⚖️ YOUR RIGHT

Medical emergency now

If you think your life or health is in danger, call 911 or go to the nearest appropriate emergency department. Do not delay emergency treatment while waiting for VA authorization.

⚠️ DEADLINE For non-VA emergency care, VA currently asks to receive notification within 72 hours after emergency care starts. The provider is preferred, but the Veteran or someone acting on the Veteran's behalf can notify VA. Late notice is not an automatic denial: VA states the claim may then be considered under unauthorized-emergency-care rules.

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE Ask the emergency department to notify VA promptly and save the visit record, discharge papers, bills, and any VA notification confirmation.

📋 CHECKLIST

Before you appeal: identify what kind of decision this is

  1. Medical treatment determination: a clinical decision about what care you should receive—medicine, therapy, treatment, or in some cases whether Community Care is medically appropriate. Use the Clinical Appeals route.
  2. Health-benefit eligibility/payment decision: eligibility for a VA health benefit, travel reimbursement, emergency-care reimbursement, certain devices, or similar benefits. Use the benefit decision-review route stated in the decision notice.
  3. Scheduling/access complaint: start with the care team/service and Patient Advocate; determine whether there is an actual clinical or benefit decision to appeal.
  4. Emergency-care payment: do not confuse prospective Community Care eligibility with reimbursement after emergency treatment.

VA denied or delayed care

  1. If the delay has become an emergency, use the emergency route above.
  2. Save referral, appointment messages, denial language, dates, names, and relevant medical records.
  3. Ask the treatment team or responsible service: what exactly was decided, by whom, and is this a medical determination or a health-benefit decision?
  4. If unresolved, contact the facility Patient Advocate. VA says the advocate can help with care concerns and, when appropriate, assist with a Clinical Appeal.
  5. If a Community Care request was denied, ask which eligibility or medical determination controlled the denial.
⚖️ YOUR RIGHT

Clinical Appeals — when you disagree with a medical treatment decision

VA's current Clinical Appeals process applies to medical treatment determinations, such as whether a provider should prescribe a medication or provide a particular therapy. It is different from appealing eligibility for a health benefit.

  1. Write the appeal: identify the treatment decision, explain why you disagree, and include medical evidence you want considered.
  2. Send it to the facility Patient Advocate as soon as you can. The Patient Advocate routes the written request through the Clinical Appeals process.
  3. Receipt: VA says you should receive a “Notice of Receipt of Clinical Appeal.”
  4. Facility review: if the appeal proceeds, the facility chief medical officer or designee reviews it with relevant records and, if needed, other experts.
  5. Final facility letter: VA sends a written decision.
  6. If still denied: you can request further review in writing by the Veterans Integrated Service Network (VISN). The VISN Patient Advocate routes that request, and the VISN chief medical officer reviews the appeal.
  7. VISN decision: VA sends a final letter or email if you elect email.

📚 AUTHORITY VA Clinical Appeals of medical treatment decisions.

Health-benefit eligibility or payment decisions

VA separately states that VHA benefit decisions—such as certain health-care eligibility, emergency-care reimbursement, travel reimbursement, or other health-benefit claims—use the benefits decision-review system rather than the Clinical Appeals route. For covered post-AMA decisions, the current VHA review page states a one-year period to initiate review, with Supplemental Claim, Higher-Level Review, and Board Appeal potentially available depending on the claimant and decision.

  1. Read the decision notice and Form 20-0998/review-rights information if provided.
  2. Use a Supplemental Claim when the route is available and new and relevant evidence is needed.
  3. Use HLR when available if the existing record is complete but you believe the decision contains an error or difference of opinion.
  4. Use Board Appeal when available if you want Veterans Law Judge review.
  5. Follow the address/submission instructions in the actual decision because VHA programs can route filings differently.

📚 AUTHORITY VHA: rights to seek review of a healthcare benefits decision.

Community Care decision tree

  1. Emergency? Use emergency-care rules, not an ordinary referral pathway.
  2. Prospective care? Ordinary Community Care generally requires VA approval before treatment except specific urgent/emergency pathways.
  3. Eligibility: under 38 CFR §17.4010, ask which criterion applies—service unavailable at VA, applicable access standards, best medical interest, quality standards, or another listed basis.
  4. Request denied? Current VA guidance says a denial of a request for Community Care medical treatment can be challenged through the Clinical Appeals process; the facility chief medical officer or designee reviews the appeal and relevant medical records.
  5. Payment denied after care? Identify whether this is an authorized-care claim, a contractual/provider dispute, or unauthorized emergency care. Those are different payment-review routes.

📚 AUTHORITY VA Community Care eligibility · 38 CFR §17.4010.

⚠️ DEADLINE

Beneficiary travel

VA's current online travel guidance says reimbursement claims generally must be submitted within 30 days of the appointment. Receipts or proof of payment are needed for claimed expenses, and meals/lodging generally require prior approval.

If VA issues a benefit decision denying travel reimbursement, treat it as a health-benefit decision and follow the review-rights notice rather than trying to use the Clinical Appeals process for a payment eligibility dispute.

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE Save the appointment evidence, BTSSS claim confirmation, mileage/address information, receipts, denial notice, and any prior authorization for lodging or meals.

📚 AUTHORITY VA travel reimbursement filing guidance.

Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers (PCAFC)

For PCAFC decisions dated on or after February 19, 2019, VA currently lists four possible review routes: Supplemental Claim, Higher-Level Review, Board Appeal, and Clinical Appeal. HLR and Board Appeal have a stated one-year deadline from the decision. Clinical Appeal allows the facility chief of staff to review the decision and permits new medical evidence.

For PCAFC decisions before February 19, 2019, VA lists different options, including the legacy PCAFC Notice of Disagreement (VA Form 10-307) or Clinical Appeal. VA Form 10-306 can be used to request information about a PCAFC decision.

📚 AUTHORITY VA Family Caregiver Program decision reviews and appeals.

Get and correct VA medical records

  1. GET THE RECORD: request the relevant VA medical record through the appropriate VA records/Privacy Act process.
  2. IDENTIFY THE ERROR: specify the exact entry, date, author, and why you believe it is inaccurate, incomplete, untimely, or irrelevant.
  3. REQUEST AMENDMENT: use VA's Privacy Act amendment process where applicable.
  4. SAVE THE RESPONSE: keep the request, proof of submission, and VA's written response.
  5. ESCALATE IF DENIED: follow the review instructions in the written response.

C-file is different: a VA disability claims file is not the same thing as ordinary VHA medical records.

⚖️ YOUR RIGHT

VA caused harm: 38 U.S.C. §1151 benefits route

Section 1151 is a VA benefits route, not a malpractice lawsuit. For modern claims, 38 CFR §3.361 requires analysis of additional disability or death, actual causation by qualifying VA care/treatment/examination, and proximate cause. Depending on the facts, proximate cause may involve VA fault or an event not reasonably foreseeable; informed-consent rules can also matter.

A bad outcome by itself does not establish §1151 entitlement.

📋 CHECKLIST Preserve pre-treatment records, consent documents, procedure notes, discharge instructions, post-treatment records, independent medical opinions, and a timeline of the claimed additional disability.

🚨 GET A LAWYER

Federal Tort Claims Act (FTCA)

FTCA is a separate tort claim against the United States. VA OGC states that it can provide money compensation for qualifying injury caused by a wrongful act or omission of a federal employee acting within the scope of federal employment, and that it does not automatically govern acts of non-VA medical facilities, community-care providers, or independent contractors.

⚠️ DEADLINE 28 U.S.C. §2401(b) generally requires presentment in writing to the appropriate federal agency within 2 years after accrual, and suit within 6 months after mailing of final denial. Accrual can be legally complex.

📄 FORM VA says SF 95 is convenient but not mandatory if written presentment otherwise satisfies the requirements. A valid claim needs a sum certain.

Administrative exhaustion: 28 U.S.C. §2675(a) generally requires agency presentment and final denial, or six months without final disposition, before suit.

If they say no

  1. Save the denial or clinical decision.
  2. Record the date received.
  3. Classify it: clinical treatment, health-benefit eligibility/payment, authorization, records, §1151, or tort liability.
  4. Use only the review route governing that category.
  5. Preserve proof and the medical record.
  6. Get representation early for §1151/FTCA or serious care-access disputes.
