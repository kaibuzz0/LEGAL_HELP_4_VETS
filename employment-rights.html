USERRA & Veteran Employment Rights

Your military service should not cost you your civilian job. USERRA may protect you from discrimination, require reemployment, and stop retaliation — but only if you act within the right deadlines and follow the right steps.

Legal Information, Not Legal Advice. This guide explains federal USERRA protections based on official sources. It does not guarantee reinstatement, back pay, or any specific outcome. Talk to a qualified attorney, DOL VETS, or ESGR for help with your specific case.

Do This First

  1. Write down what happened. Dates, conversations, emails, orders, and any adverse action.
  2. Collect your military orders and service records. Include DD-214, drill schedules, deployment orders, and dates.
  3. Save employer communications. Emails, texts, termination letters, schedule changes, and performance reviews.
  4. Identify the exact problem. Refusal to rehire, firing, demotion, harassment, retaliation, or benefits denial?
  5. Contact the right help channel quickly. DOL VETS and ESGR can assist; deadlines matter.

What Is USERRA?

The Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act (USERRA) is a federal law that gives servicemembers and veterans certain employment and reemployment rights. It protects against discrimination, requires prompt reemployment after qualifying service, and prohibits retaliation for asserting USERRA rights. [141]

USERRA applies to nearly all employers in the United States, including private employers, State and local governments, and the Federal government. It does not cover every workplace problem involving a veteran, and it does not guarantee every unfavorable action can be reversed. [142]

Who USERRA May Protect

USERRA protects individuals who perform duty, have performed duty, or have applied to perform duty in a uniformed service. This includes members of the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, Space Force, Coast Guard, Reserves, National Guard, and certain other uniformed services when called to federal service. [142]

  • Servicemember: a person currently serving in the uniformed services.
  • Reservist or National Guard member: called to active duty, training, or drill.
  • Veteran: a person who previously served and is seeking reemployment or asserting rights after service.
  • Applicants: USERRA also protects people from discrimination because they might serve.

Important: Not everyone who served automatically qualifies for every USERRA protection. Eligibility depends on the type of service, notice, timing, and the employer's status.

Important Return-to-Work Deadlines

USERRA's return-to-work deadlines depend on the length of military service. There is not one universal deadline. [143]

Service lengthGeneral return/application deadlineImportant note
1 to 30 days Return at the beginning of the first full regularly scheduled work period after the end of service, plus safe travel time and an 8-hour rest period This is the most common short-duty rule. Verify your employer's schedule and any applicable orders.
31 to 180 days Apply for reemployment no later than 14 days after completing service If it is impossible or unreasonable to apply within 14 days, apply as soon as possible.
More than 180 days Apply for reemployment no later than 90 days after completing service Longer deployments generally trigger the 90-day application window.

If you are hospitalized or recovering from an illness or injury incurred in or aggravated during service, these deadlines may be extended for a period related to convalescence, with some limits. [143]

If you are unsure which deadline applies: document the dates immediately and contact DOL VETS or ESGR. Do not rely on guesswork.

Notice to Your Employer

USERRA generally requires advance notice of military service when possible, but the law recognizes circumstances where advance notice is impossible, impracticable, or contrary to military requirements. [143]

  • Before service: Give written or oral notice as far in advance as military requirements allow.
  • After service: Report back or apply for reemployment within the applicable deadline based on service length.
  • Document it: Keep copies of emails, texts, voicemails, or any other evidence showing when and how you gave notice.
  • No perfect form required: Notice does not have to be on a special form. Any clear communication about the service can suffice.
Reemployment Rights in Plain Language

If you meet USERRA's eligibility and timing requirements, your employer must generally return you to the job and benefits you would have had if you had not left for military service — often called the "escalator" principle. [144]

  • Same or similar job: You are entitled to reemployment in the position you would have held with reasonable certainty if your employment had not been interrupted by military service.
  • Seniority and status: You generally keep the seniority, status, and rate of pay you would have obtained by continuous employment.
  • Benefits: Employer benefits, pension credits, and other employment rights generally continue to accrue as if you had remained continuously employed.
  • Exceptions exist: If the employer's circumstances changed so much that reemployment is impossible or unreasonable, or if you are no longer qualified, the law may allow different treatment. [143]
Anti-Discrimination and Retaliation

USERRA prohibits an employer from denying initial employment, reemployment, retention, promotion, or any benefit of employment because of a person's membership, application for membership, performance of service, application for service, or obligation to serve in the uniformed services. Retaliation against someone who exercises a USERRA right or assists another person is also prohibited. [142]

This does not mean every bad workplace decision is illegal. The adverse action must be connected to your military status, service, or USERRA rights. Document the connection carefully.

Health Insurance, Pension, and Other Benefits

USERRA contains specific rules about employer health plans, pension plans, and other benefits during and after military service. [145] [146] [156]

  • Health plan continuation:You may elect to continue employer health coverage during military service under certain conditions and cost rules. The exact duration and cost depend on the plan and applicable USERRA regulations; check your employer's plan documents.
  • Reinstatement of coverage: Upon reemployment, health plan coverage is generally reinstated without waiting periods or exclusions.
  • Pension: Pension benefits generally must treat the period of military service as continuous employment for purposes of participation, vesting, and accrual.
  • Vacation/seniority:> USERRA generally protects seniority and status; it does not necessarily require the employer to pay you for military leave or to give you paid vacation you did not earn.
Employer Defenses / Exceptions

USERRA rights are not absolute. An employer may raise specific defenses. The burden and specific rules depend on the claim type. [143]

  • Changed circumstances: The employer's situation may have changed so much that reemployment is impossible or unreasonable.
  • Cumulative service limits: There is a limit on the total cumulative length of service with one employer for which reemployment rights apply.
  • Failure to give timely notice: Except where notice was impossible or not required, missing return deadlines can affect rights.
  • Not qualified: If you are no longer qualified for the position and cannot become qualified with reasonable efforts, reemployment may be to a different position.
  • Disqualifying discharge: A discharge under other than honorable conditions may affect reemployment rights.
The Five-Year Service Limit

USERRA generally limits reemployment rights to a cumulative total of five years of service with the same employer. Some types of service do not count toward this limit, including certain involuntary active-duty extensions, National Guard duty under specific authorities, and service that is required to complete an initial period of obligated service. [157]

  • Count carefully: Add up all periods of service with the same employer that count toward the limit.
  • Check exceptions: Some service is excluded by law; a DOL VETS or ESGR representative can help determine what counts.
  • Do not assume: The exact calculation depends on your service history and the statutory exceptions.

Where to Get Help: DOL VETS, ESGR, and DOJ

USERRA enforcement usually begins with DOL VETS. ESGR can provide informal mediation. The Department of Justice may become involved in certain enforcement actions referred by DOL VETS. [147] [148] [149]

  • DOL VETS: The federal agency that administers USERRA assistance, accepts complaints from servicemembers and veterans, investigates, and may refer cases to DOJ. Use DOL's elaws USERRA Advisor or contact VETS directly. [151] [152]
  • ESGR: Employer Support of the Guard and Reserve offers informal help, education, and ombudsman/mediation services to resolve USERRA disputes without a formal complaint. [154]
  • DOJ Civil Rights Division: The Department of Justice may file suit to enforce USERRA against State and private employers after referral from DOL VETS. Individuals may also have a private right of action. [153]
  • VA Crisis Line: Job loss and employment stress can become a crisis. If you need immediate support, call or text 988 and press 1, or chat at veteranscrisisline.net.

Sample Letters

Use these as starting points. Replace bracketed text with your information. Keep copies and proof of delivery.

Sample 1: Request for Reemployment

Dear [Employer/HR Department]:

I am writing to request reemployment under the Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act (USERRA), 38 U.S.C. Chapter 43. I left my position of [JOB TITLE] on [DATE] to perform service in the [BRANCH/UNIT] and completed that service on [DATE].

I am available to return to work at the beginning of the next regularly scheduled work period, allowing for safe travel and rest as required. Please confirm in writing when I should report back and to what position.

Attached are copies of my military orders and service documentation. Please let me know if you need additional information.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
[Contact Information]
[Date]

Sample 2: USERRA Rights Concern

Dear [Employer/HR Department]:

I believe my [adverse action, e.g., termination/demotion/schedule reduction] on [DATE] may be connected to my military service / USERRA rights. I have performed service with the [BRANCH/UNIT], including [brief description, e.g., annual training / deployment].

I am requesting that [employer] review this matter and respond in writing explaining the reason for the action. I ask that you preserve all relevant records, including emails, personnel files, and schedules related to this decision.

I hope we can resolve this matter promptly. I am also available to discuss it.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
[Contact Information]
[Date]

Sample 3: Request for Written Explanation

Dear [Employer/HR Department]:

On [DATE] I was informed that [describe adverse action, e.g., I was terminated, not rehired, or denied a promotion]. I am requesting a written explanation of the reason for this action, including any policies, dates, and decision-makers involved.

Please provide this explanation within a reasonable time. I am preserving all related records and ask that you do the same.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
[Contact Information]
[Date]

Documents to Save

Gather these documents if they apply to your case. Only provide them to the appropriate agency or representative when requested.

  • Military orders, DD-214, and service records
  • Drill schedules, annual training orders, mobilization/deployment orders
  • Employer emails, texts, letters, and memos
  • Termination, demotion, or disciplinary documents
  • Pay stubs, benefits enrollment records, and pension records
  • Performance reviews and personnel records
  • Work schedules before and after military service
  • Dates of notice to employer and dates of return/application
  • Copies of anything you submit to your employer or an agency
  • Witness contact information if coworkers observed relevant events
Common Terms
USERRA
Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act — the federal law protecting servicemembers' civilian employment and reemployment rights. [141]
Uniformed services
The armed forces, reserves, National Guard when in federal service, and certain other uniformed services covered by USERRA.
Escalator position
The job, seniority, status, and pay you would have reached if your employment had continued without interruption for military service. [144]
Reemployment right
The right to return to civilian employment after qualifying military service, if all conditions and deadlines are met. [143]
DOL VETS
Department of Labor Veterans' Employment and Training Service — the federal agency that provides USERRA assistance and investigates complaints. [147]
ESGR
Employer Support of the Guard and Reserve — provides informal help and ombudsman/mediation for USERRA disputes. [154]
Private right of action
The ability to file your own lawsuit to enforce USERRA rights, separate from a government enforcement action. [149]
