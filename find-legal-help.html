Find the Right Legal Help

Last legally verified: 2026-08-27
Official sources checked: VA OGC Accreditation Search; VA LSV-H; VA/DOL/DOJ public guidance where applicable.
State-specific warning: housing, criminal, consumer, family, and immigration representation depends on jurisdiction and provider eligibility.
⚖️ YOUR RIGHT

VA benefits claim or review

Use a VA-accredited representative. The official VA OGC search lists currently accredited attorneys, claims agents, VSO representatives, and recognized VSOs.

Search VA-accredited representatives.

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE Verify the person in the official search before relying on an online advertisement. VSO representation is generally provided without a fee; attorney/agent fee rules depend on the stage and governing VA rules, so review the fee agreement and accreditation status.

🚨 GET A LAWYER

CAVC appeal

An appeal from a final Board of Veterans' Appeals decision to the U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims is a court matter, not another ordinary VA review lane. Use an attorney or organization that actually practices before the CAVC. The Veterans Consortium Pro Bono Program screens certain cases for possible free representation; acceptance is not guaranteed.

Veterans Consortium Pro Bono Program.

Homelessness, eviction, foreclosure, or housing instability

⚖️ YOUR RIGHT VA's LSV-H grant program funds legal services for eligible Veterans who are homeless or at risk for homelessness. Current VA guidance says services may include housing, family law, income support, certain criminal-defense matters symptomatic of homelessness, discharge upgrades, domestic-violence matters, access to health care, consumer law, and employment law.

Important: each grantee's actual scope and capacity differs. A grantee listed for “housing” may handle eviction defense but not every housing case.

Current VA LSV-H program and grantee referrals.

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE Also ask SSVF and local legal aid whether they can help with the housing problem. LSV-H and SSVF are separate programs.

USERRA / employment

For military-service reemployment or discrimination issues, use the USERRA guide and consider DOL VETS, ESGR for appropriate employer/employee assistance, and a qualified employment attorney. DOJ involvement is not automatic; it depends on the enforcement route and case.

📋 CHECKLIST Save orders, notices to the employer, service dates, job description, termination/reemployment messages, pay records, benefits records, and a timeline.

Discharge upgrade or military-record correction

Use a veterans legal clinic or attorney experienced in military administrative law when the case is complex. The correct board and form depend on the requested relief: DRB and BCMR/BCNR are not interchangeable. See Discharge / Record Correction.

Consumer debt, garnishment, repossession, or fraud

Use a consumer attorney or legal-aid program that practices in your state. If the debt is a VA overpayment, also use VA Debt. If housing instability is involved, an LSV-H grantee may be able to provide certain consumer-law services, subject to provider scope.

Immigration

Use a qualified immigration attorney or recognized/accredited immigration legal-service provider familiar with military families. Do not rely on “notarios” or unlicensed immigration consultants for legal representation. Military service can affect some immigration routes, but eligibility is fact-specific.

Family law / protective orders

Custody, divorce, support, guardianship, and protective-order law is state-specific. Use local legal aid or a licensed family-law attorney. Military legal-assistance eligibility is not universal for every former servicemember after discharge; confirm eligibility with the installation or service office before relying on it.

If homelessness or housing risk is connected to the family problem, check LSV-H because current program rules allow some family-law and domestic/intimate-partner-violence legal services.

🚨 GET A LAWYER

VA medical harm / FTCA

For possible FTCA claims, get counsel early because administrative presentment, accrual, sum-certain requirements, exhaustion, and court deadlines can permanently affect the claim. See FTCA. For a VA-benefits route under 38 U.S.C. §1151, see §1151.

Before you hire or authorize anyone

  1. Confirm the representative practices in the right area of law.
  2. For VA benefits representation, verify current VA accreditation in the official OGC search.
  3. Ask what the representation covers and what it does not cover.
  4. Get fee terms in writing when fees may be charged.
  5. Do not assume a referral program guarantees acceptance.
  6. Keep a copy of any representation agreement and every deadline already running.
