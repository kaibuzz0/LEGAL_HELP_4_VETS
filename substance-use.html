SUBSTANCE USE AND RECOVERY

Substance use can be a medical crisis, a legal crisis, or both. This section tells veterans and families how to get immediate help, access VA treatment, protect benefits, and understand treatment courts.

What This Is and Who Qualifies

This page helps veterans and families find substance-use treatment, crisis support, and legal pathways when substance use is connected to service trauma, PTSD, TBI, or a bad discharge. You do not need to be in VA treatment already to get help. [3] [61]

Who qualifies: Any veteran or active-duty servicemember in crisis can call the Veterans Crisis Line. For VA SUD treatment, veterans generally may need to be enrolled in VA health care or eligible for enrollment. If VA cannot provide timely treatment, the MISSION Act may allow community care. [1] [4] [61]

Veterans Crisis Line + SAMHSA: When and What to Say

If you or a veteran you know is thinking about suicide, using substances in a way that feels out of control, or in immediate danger, call now. [1] [2]

How to Enroll in VA Substance Use Treatment

VA provides detox, residential, outpatient, and medication-assisted treatment (MAT) for substance use disorders. [61] [3]

  1. Call your local VA medical center and ask for the “Substance Use Disorder clinic” or “Mental Health intake.” [3]
  2. If not enrolled in VA health care, apply with VA Form 10-10EZ or online. Bring your DD-214 and income/asset information. [48]
  3. Tell the intake clinician: “I want treatment for substance use. I also have [PTSD/TBI/chronic pain/service trauma] and I think they are connected.”
  4. Ask what level of care is available: detox, residential, intensive outpatient, standard outpatient, or MAT (such as buprenorphine or naltrexone). [61]
  5. If VA cannot schedule treatment within a reasonable time, ask for a MISSION Act community care referral. [4] [62]

MISSION Act Community Care Referral for SUD

If VA cannot provide timely, accessible SUD treatment in your area, you may qualify for community care under the MISSION Act. [4] [62]

  1. Ask your VA primary care or mental health provider for a referral to community care.
  2. VA will check: is there a VA provider within 30 minutes/60 minutes drive? Is the wait time acceptable? [4]
  3. If approved, VA will authorize a community provider and coordinate payment.
  4. If denied, request a formal denial in writing and contact the VA patient advocate. [40]

38 C.F.R. § 3.301: Substance Use Is Not may be available through a Bar to Compensation

VA regulations say that substance abuse is not a basis for a compensation claim by itself. However, if substance use is secondary to a service-connected mental health or physical condition, or if the underlying condition was caused by service, compensation may still be possible. [5]

  1. File a claim for the primary service-connected condition (PTSD, depression, chronic pain, TBI). [17]
  2. Ask your treating provider to document the connection: “The veteran’s substance use began/worsened as a result of [service-connected PTSD/TBI/pain].”
  3. Use VA Form 21-4138 for buddy statements and your own lay statement describing the connection. [18]
  4. If VA denies for “willful misconduct,” request a Higher-Level Review or Supplemental Claim with a medical nexus opinion. [20] [19]

Veterans Treatment Courts: How to Find and Ask for One

Veterans Treatment Courts (VTCs) are specialized courts that connect justice-involved veterans to treatment and mentorship instead of traditional punishment. Not every jurisdiction has one. [6] [63]

  1. Search the Justice For Vets directory or ask your local court clerk if your county has a VTC. [63]
  2. If you already have an attorney or public defender, ask: “Can I be screened for Veterans Treatment Court? I am a veteran and I need treatment, not just punishment.” [6]
  3. Eligibility usually requires a military discharge (any character), a connection between the offense and service-related issues, and willingness to follow treatment and court requirements.
  4. A VA justice outreach specialist, VSO, or treatment court mentor can help with screening.

If your area has no VTC: ask the judge or prosecutor for alternative sentencing that includes VA SUD treatment and mental health care. [6]

If Substance Use Led to a Bad Discharge

Substance use connected to PTSD, TBI, MST, or another in-service condition can be the basis for a discharge upgrade. See the Discharge Upgrade page for full steps. [29] [39]

  1. Get service treatment records showing any in-service mental health or physical trauma.
  2. Get post-service records diagnosing PTSD, TBI, depression, or substance use disorder.
  3. File DD Form 149 or DD Form 293 explaining that the misconduct was caused or worsened by a service-connected condition.
  4. File a VA claim at the same time to trigger a Character of Discharge review. [38] [84]

Family and Caregiver Support

Spouses, partners, parents, and adult children can get their own support while helping a veteran through recovery. [64] [2]

  • VA Caregiver Support Program: Call 1-855-260-3274 or contact your VA Caregiver Support Coordinator. [64]
  • SAMHSA family helpline: 1-800-662-HELP (4357) for local family support resources. [2]
  • Al-Anon / Nar-Anon: peer support groups for family members (verify local meetings). [2]
  • Keep a crisis plan: Write down the veteran’s medications, providers, triggers, and the Veterans Crisis Line number where family can find it quickly.

Document Checklist for SUD-Related Claims

  • Service records showing in-service injury, trauma, or mental health episode
  • VA and private treatment records for substance use and mental health
  • Nexus statement from a provider linking substance use to service-connected condition
  • Buddy statements from fellow service members or family
  • VA Form 21-526ez, 21-4138, 10-10EZ, or discharge upgrade forms as needed
  • Call log of every VA/SAMHSA/community care contact

Immediate Crisis: Veterans Crisis Line and SAMHSA

If you or a veteran you love is in crisis, call now. These services are confidential and free.

  • Veterans Crisis Line: Call 988 then press 1, text 838255, or chat online. [1]
  • SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357. Free, confidential, 24/7 treatment referral. [2]
Script: "My name is [name]. I am a veteran [or I am calling for a veteran]. I am struggling with [substance use / alcohol / opioid use / crisis]. I need help finding treatment right away. I am in [city, state] and [have / do not have] VA health care."

What happens next: They will assess safety, offer de-escalation, and connect you to local VA or community treatment. If you are in immediate danger, they may involve emergency services.

Enroll in VA Substance Use Treatment

VA provides substance use disorder (SUD) treatment including detox, residential rehabilitation, outpatient care, and medication-assisted treatment. [61]

  1. If not already enrolled in VA health care, apply with VA Form 10-10EZ. [48]
  2. Call your VA primary care provider or the mental health clinic. Say: "I need an evaluation for substance use treatment."
  3. Attend the SUD evaluation. Be honest about substances used, frequency, and any withdrawal history.
  4. Ask for the treatment plan: detox, residential, intensive outpatient, standard outpatient, or medication-assisted treatment (MAT).
  5. Ask about coordination with mental health care if PTSD, depression, or anxiety are also present.
  6. If VA cannot provide timely treatment, ask for a MISSION Act community care referral. [62]

How to Request Community Care Under the MISSION Act

If VA cannot provide SUD treatment within a reasonable time or at a reasonable distance, you may be eligible for community care. [4]

  1. Ask your VA provider for a referral to community care. Say: "I want a community care referral for substance use treatment because [VA wait time / distance / no available program]."
  2. VA will review whether you meet access standards (drive time / wait time).
  3. If approved, VA will authorize a community provider and coordinate payment.
  4. Keep the authorization paperwork and confirm billing with the community provider.

38 C.F.R. 3.301: Substance Use Is Not may be available through a Bar

VA regulations state that alcohol or drug abuse secondary to a service-connected condition can itself be service connected. Even where substance use is the primary cause, veterans may still be entitled to compensation for conditions caused or worsened by service. [5]

Key point: Do not assume a substance use history destroys your VA claim. A VSO or attorney can help argue that your substance use was secondary to service-connected PTSD, TBI, or another condition, or that the claim should be decided on other medical evidence.

Veterans Treatment Courts (VTC)

Veterans Treatment Courts combine court supervision with treatment and mentoring for eligible veterans facing criminal charges. [6]

  1. Find out if your jurisdiction has a VTC. Ask your attorney or public defender, the court clerk, or search "veterans treatment court [county]."
  2. If eligible, ask your attorney to request VTC consideration at the earliest hearing.
  3. Be prepared to provide: DD-214, VA treatment records, current diagnoses, and a treatment plan.
  4. VTC usually requires regular court appearances, treatment attendance, and mentorship.
  5. Successful completion may reduce or dismiss charges in some cases. Outcomes vary by jurisdiction.

Important: Not every county has a VTC. Verify locally and ask your attorney whether it is an option.

If Substance Use Led to a Bad Discharge

If substance use or related behavior resulted in an Other Than Honorable or other unfavorable discharge, you may still have paths to VA benefits and a discharge upgrade.

  1. File a VA claim for any service-connected condition (PTSD, TBI, etc.) to trigger a Character of Discharge review. [38]
  2. Get a current mental health diagnosis and treatment records.
  3. Consider a discharge upgrade application under the PTSD/TBI pathway if your discharge was related to undiagnosed mental health or substance use. [29]

Support for Family and Caregivers

Family members do not have to do this alone. VA and community resources support caregivers. [64]

  • VA Caregiver Support Program offers education, respite care, and mental health resources.
  • SAMHSA has family support resources and treatment locators.
  • Al-Anon and Nar-Anon are free peer support groups for families.
  • If the veteran refuses help, call the Veterans Crisis Line for guidance on how to keep them safe.

Document Checklist for Substance Use-Related Claims

  • DD-214 and service records showing in-service trauma or injury
  • VA and private mental health / SUD treatment records
  • Nexus opinion linking substance use or related condition to service
  • Lay statements from family or fellow servicemembers
  • Arrest/court records if VTC or upgrade is being pursued
