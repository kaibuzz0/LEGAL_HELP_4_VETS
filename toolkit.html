ADVOCATE TOOLKIT

This toolkit gives you forms, templates, and trackers to organize a veteran's case, communicate with agencies, and never lose a deadline.

Case Summary Template

Use this one-page summary for every appointment with a VSO, attorney, or congressional office:

  • Veteran: Name, last 4 SSN, branch, dates of service, discharge status, VA file number
  • Issue: One-sentence problem statement
  • History: Dates of claims, appeals, exams, and decisions
  • Evidence: Submitted and missing evidence
  • Goal: What you want the agency to do
  • Next Deadline: Date and action required

Letter Templates

Records request:

"Dear Records Officer: I authorize the release of my complete medical records, including [specific records], to [recipient name/address]. My identifying information: [full name, DOB, dates of service, patient ID]. Please send the records within 20 business days. [Signature, date]."

VSO appointment prep email:

"I need help with [specific issue]. I have attached my DD-214, decision letters, and a case summary. My questions: 1) What form do I file? 2) What evidence am I missing? 3) What is my deadline? 4) Can you represent me?"

Congressional inquiry: [41]

"I am a constituent and veteran writing to request your office's help with a federal agency issue. I have been trying to resolve [issue] with [agency] since [date]. Enclosed is a case summary, copies of correspondence, and my contact information. Please have your caseworker contact me."

Full Document Checklist

  • DD-214 and discharge paperwork
  • Service Treatment Records (STRs)
  • Personnel records (assignments, awards, disciplinary)
  • Private medical records
  • VA treatment notes, C&P exams, decision letters
  • Employment records (for TDIU / lost wages)
  • Lay and buddy statements (VA Form 21-4138) [18]
  • Nexus letters from medical providers
  • Financial records (for pension or hardship claims)

What to Do If an Agency Says No

  • Ask for the denial or refusal in writing with the specific reason.
  • Identify the appeal lane or supervisor escalation path.
  • Check the deadline — one year for most VA appeals, but other agencies may be shorter.
  • Get help from a VSO, attorney, or congressional office if the issue is complex.
  • Update your timeline tracker with every new deadline.
YOUR NEXT ACTION: Create a case summary for the veteran you are helping. List the current issue, missing evidence, and next deadline. Then pick the right claim or appeal lane.
