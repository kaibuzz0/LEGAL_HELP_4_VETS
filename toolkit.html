What This Is and Who Qualifies

The Advocate Toolkit is a set of forms, templates, and trackers you can use to organize a veteran’s case and communicate clearly with VA, employers, landlords, creditors, and elected representatives. [?]

Who qualifies: Veterans representing themselves, family members helping a veteran, accredited VSO representatives, attorneys, and caseworkers. [57]

Letter Templates

1. VSO Representative Prep Letter

Dear [VSO Representative],

I am requesting your help with the following VA matter:
Veteran: [Name, last 4 SSN]
Branch of service: [Branch, dates]
Issue: [Denied claim / appeal / discharge upgrade / etc.]
Date of VA decision: [Date]
Deadline: [Date]

I have attached: [DD-214 / decision letter / medical records / nexus letter / buddy statements].

Please contact me at [phone/email] to schedule an appointment.

Sincerely, [Name]

Send with: VA Form 21-22 or 21-22a if you want the representative to access your VA file. [13]

2. Congressional Inquiry Request

The Honorable [Representative / Senator name]
[Office address]

Dear [Title] [Last name],

I am a constituent writing to request assistance with a federal agency matter. I am a veteran [or I am assisting veteran [name]]. The issue involves [VA / DFAS / USCIS / another agency].

Summary: [One paragraph explaining the problem, dates, and agency involved.]

Action requested: [“Please ask VA to expedite this claim,” “Please request a status update,” etc.]

I have attached a signed privacy release, a case summary, and copies of relevant documents. Please contact me at [phone/email].

Sincerely, [Name, full address, phone, email]

Important: Include a signed privacy release form from the representative’s office. Congress cannot overturn agency decisions but can get status and answers. [41]

3. Records Request to Private Provider

[Provider name and address]

Re: Medical records for [Patient name, DOB, dates of treatment]

Under HIPAA, I request a complete copy of my medical records from [start date] to [end date], including office notes, test results, imaging reports, and prescription records. Please provide the records in electronic or paper format. If you charge a fee, please notify me before processing.

I authorize release to: [Myself / My attorney / My accredited representative].

Sincerely, [Name, signature, date]

For VA records: Use VA Form 20-10206 for the C-file or VA medical records release forms for specific facilities. [23]

Full Document Checklist for Any Veteran Case

  • DD-214 (all copies) and any discharge upgrade decisions
  • Service treatment records and personnel file
  • VA C-file (request with VA Form 20-10206)
  • VA and private medical records
  • All VA decision letters and rating code sheets
  • Nexus letters and expert opinions
  • Buddy and family statements
  • Employment records and TDIU employer forms (21-4192)
  • Marriage, birth, and death certificates for survivor claims
  • Copies of every form submitted, with dates and tracking numbers
  • Call log and timeline tracker

What If the Toolkit Is Not Enough

  • Find an accredited representative: Use the VA OGC accreditation search. [57]
  • Legal aid: Contact your state or county bar association for low-cost or pro bono veteran legal clinics. [13]
  • Crisis: If the veteran is unsafe, call 988 and press 1, or text 988. [1]

Case Summary Template

Use this one-page summary for every appointment with a VSO, attorney, or congressional office:

  • Veteran: Name, last 4 SSN, branch, dates of service, discharge status, VA file number
  • Issue: One-sentence problem statement
  • History: Dates of claims, appeals, exams, and decisions

What to Do If an Agency Says No

  1. Ask for the denial or refusal in writing with the specific reason.
  2. Identify the appeal lane or supervisor escalation path.
  3. Check the deadline — one year for most VA appeals, but other agencies may be shorter.
  4. Get help from a VSO, attorney, or congressional office if the issue is complex.
  5. Update your timeline tracker with every new deadline.
YOUR NEXT ACTION: Create a case summary for the veteran you are helping. List the current issue, missing evidence, and next deadline. Then pick the right claim or appeal lane.

Step-by-Step: How to Organize a Veteran Case from Day One

  1. Gather identity and service documents. Start with the DD-214, Social Security card, and VA decision letters. Without these, every agency will ask you to come back. [13]
  2. Request the C-file. Use VA Form 20-10206 to get VA’s complete claims file. It shows what VA has and what is missing. [23]
  3. Build a case summary. Use the template below so every call starts with the same one-page facts.
  4. Choose the right lane or program. Use this site’s pages to decide: claim, appeal, discharge upgrade, survivor benefit, housing, etc.
  5. File the right form and start a call log. Send certified mail when possible, keep copies, and log every phone call.
  6. Track deadlines. One year for most VA appeals; 15 years for DRB discharge upgrades; variable for survivor benefits. Calendar early. [78]
  7. Escalate if stuck. Contact a VSO, VA patient advocate, congressional office, or accredited attorney. [13] [40] [41]

Common Myths That Stop Veterans from Acting

You may have heardThe real ruleWhat to do
“VA said I make too much money.” VA disability compensation has no income limit. Pension does, but medical expenses can reduce countable income. [90] Apply for disability compensation. For pension, list all medical expenses.
“Bad discharge means I can never get benefits.” VA must do a Character of Discharge review. Some discharges are upgradeable through DRB/BCMR. [38] [82] File a claim anyway and apply for a discharge upgrade in parallel.
“I missed the deadline, so it is over.” Deadlines can sometimes be extended for VA error, new evidence, or protective claims. Even past deadlines may have paths. [78] [22] File a Supplemental Claim or new Intent to File immediately and ask a VSO.
“My doctor said it is not service-connected.” One doctor’s opinion is not final. VA uses the totality of evidence. The right nexus language matters more than the doctor’s title. [18] [75] Get a private nexus opinion and a buddy statement. Use VA Form 21-4138.
“I have to hire a lawyer.” VSOs and accredited claims agents help for free. Attorneys can help complex cases but are not required for most initial claims. [57] Start with a VSO. Use an attorney for appeals to CAVC or complex immigration/removal cases.
