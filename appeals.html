APPEALS NAVIGATION

When VA denies a claim or gives a lower rating than you expected, you have three main lanes to challenge the decision. Choose the right lane based on what went wrong and what evidence you have.

Three Lanes: When to Use Each

Deadline Protection: Do Not Miss One Year

For most VA decisions, you have one year from the decision date to file a Supplemental Claim, Higher-Level Review, or Notice of Disagreement. Miss the deadline and the decision becomes final.

Step 1: Find the date on the first page of your VA decision letter. Step 2: Add one year. Step 3: File before that date. If the deadline falls on a weekend or federal holiday, file the previous business day. Use certified mail or VA.gov so you have proof.

Protective claims: Even a short letter saying "I intend to appeal the [date] decision on [condition]" can preserve your deadline if received before the one-year mark.

Get Your C-File (VA Form 20-10206)

Your C-file contains every document VA has about you. You need it to find errors and build an appeal. [23]

  1. Download VA Form 20-10206.
  2. Check the box for "Claims File (C-File)" and any other records.
  3. Mail to the VA Evidence Intake Center or upload via VA.gov.
  4. Allow several weeks to months for delivery.
  5. Review the C-file with a VSO or attorney before choosing an appeal lane.

Sample Appeal Arguments

Inadequate C&P exam:

"The [date] C&P exam is inadequate because the examiner failed to review [specific records], did not perform [specific test], and did not explain how [medical finding] affects [condition]. A new examination is required."

Benefit of the doubt: [77]

"The evidence is in approximate balance. Under 38 U.S.C. 5107(b), VA must resolve reasonable doubt in the veteran's favor. The in-service event, current diagnosis, and attached lay statements support service connection."

Staged ratings: [78]

"The veteran's symptoms have worsened since the original rating. A staged rating is warranted to reflect the severity of [condition] during the period from [date] to [date], supported by [treatment records / exams]."

Secondary conditions:

"The veteran's [secondary condition] was caused by the service-connected [primary condition], as confirmed by Dr. [Name] in the attached nexus letter. Service connection on a secondary basis is warranted."

Duty to assist: [37]

"Under 38 C.F.R. 3.159, VA has a duty to assist in obtaining identified relevant records. VA did not obtain [specific records] that the veteran identified. The case should be remanded for development."

New Evidence Rules Per Lane

  • Supplemental Claim (20-0995): Attach new and relevant evidence. Evidence must be new (not previously submitted) and relevant (relates to the issue). [19]
  • Higher-Level Review (20-0996): No new evidence allowed. Request an informal conference to explain the error. [20]
  • Board — Direct Review (10182): No new evidence; judge reviews the existing record. [21]
  • Board — Evidence Submission: Submit new evidence within 90 days after filing the NOD.
  • Board — Hearing: Present testimony and evidence at the hearing.

Remand, Grant, or Denial

  • Grant: VA or the Board agrees. You receive a new rating/effective date and retroactive pay.
  • Denial: The decision is upheld. You may file a Supplemental Claim with new evidence or appeal further if a lane remains.
  • Remand: The Board sends the case back to VA for more development. Common. Delays final decision but can lead to a grant.

Records to Keep (Appeals Tracker)

  • VA decision letter and rating code sheet
  • Copies of submitted appeal forms (20-0995, 20-0996, 10182)
  • Certified mail receipts or VA.gov confirmations
  • New evidence submitted and date submitted
  • C-file contents index
  • Call log with VA and VSO
YOUR NEXT ACTION: Read your decision letter. Identify the deadline. If you have new evidence, file Supplemental Claim (20-0995). If VA made a clear error, file Higher-Level Review (20-0996). Do not miss the one-year deadline.
