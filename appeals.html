Fight a VA Denial: Pick the Right Review Lane

DO THIS FIRST

Last legally verified: 2026-08-27
Primary authorities checked: 38 CFR §§ 3.2500, 3.2501; VA Forms 20-0995, 20-0996, 10182, 20-0998; current VA decision-review guidance.
Warning: proposed reductions, severance, debts, and some other adverse actions have separate procedures. See Benefit Changes.
⚠️ DEADLINE

When a VA decision arrives

  1. Find the date on the decision notice.
  2. Read the review-rights notice included with that decision.
  3. Calendar the exact deadline shown there.
  4. Choose an authorized review option.
  5. Use the prescribed VA form or VA online process.
  6. Keep a complete copy and proof VA received it.

Do not rely on a generic “protective appeal,” “intent to appeal,” or informal letter to preserve an Appeals Modernization Act review deadline.

Choose the review option

OptionUse it whenEvidenceForm
Supplemental ClaimYou have new and relevant evidence, or another qualifying basis for readjudication.New and relevant evidence is generally required.📄 FORM VA Form 20-0995
Higher-Level ReviewYou want a higher-level reviewer to review the existing record for an error or difference of opinion.No new evidence.📄 FORM VA Form 20-0996
Board AppealYou want a Veterans Law Judge to review the issue.Depends on docket.📄 FORM VA Form 10182
⚠️ DEADLINE

One year is common — but not universal

Higher-Level Review and Board Appeal are generally elected within one year of the covered decision notice. A Supplemental Claim may generally be filed later, but filing within the applicable continuous-pursuit period can be important to an earlier effective date. Simultaneously contested claims use shorter rules.

Follow the deadline printed in your actual decision notice.

📋 CHECKLIST

Build your review packet before you file

  • Decision notice and review-rights notice.
  • List each issue you disagree with; do not assume one form entry automatically covers unrelated issues.
  • Write down the factual or legal reason VA gave for each denial or rating.
  • Identify the evidence VA listed and any favorable findings in the decision.
  • If using a Supplemental Claim, identify exactly what evidence is new and relevant to the reason for denial.
  • If using HLR, identify errors using the existing record; do not send new evidence as if HLR can consider it.
  • If using a Board evidence or hearing docket, calendar the applicable evidence window.

Supplemental Claim — filing machinery

  1. Use the current VA Form 20-0995 or current online process.
  2. Identify the specific prior decision/issue being challenged.
  3. Submit new and relevant evidence or identify records VA can obtain when the current procedure permits it.
  4. Explain how the evidence answers the actual reason for the prior denial. A stack of unrelated records is not a substitute for relevance.
  5. Keep the complete packet and receipt/confirmation.

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE If the missing element was nexus, diagnosis, severity, in-service event, dependency fact, or another specific requirement, organize the submission around that missing element.

Higher-Level Review — filing machinery

  1. Use current VA Form 20-0996 or the current online process where available.
  2. Identify the decision and issues for HLR.
  3. List the factual or legal errors you believe the existing record shows.
  4. If useful, request the optional informal conference on the form.
  5. Prepare a short error outline before the conference. The conference is not a hearing and does not allow new evidence.
  6. Keep the filed form and confirmation.

CUE is not the HLR standard. A veteran does not need to prove clear and unmistakable error merely to request Higher-Level Review.

Board Appeal — choose the docket intentionally

DocketEvidence strategy
Direct ReviewNo hearing and no new evidence. Use it when the argument is that the allowed record already supports a different result.
Evidence SubmissionNew evidence may be submitted with the appeal or within the applicable 90-day window after VA receives the Board Appeal.
HearingTestimony plus evidence at the hearing or within the applicable post-hearing evidence window.

🛠️ PRACTICAL MOVE Pick the docket based on whether you actually need evidence or testimony, not on old processing-time promises.

Switching review options

Current VA guidance allows switching in some circumstances, but the requirements depend on which review is pending. For example, switching away from a pending review can require withdrawal and a new review request; Board docket changes have their own timing and eligibility conditions.

  1. Check whether the current review has already been decided.
  2. Check whether evidence has been submitted or a hearing held if a Board Appeal is involved.
  3. Use current VA switching instructions rather than mailing an informal “protective” letter.
  4. Consider effective-date consequences before switching after one year.

Get the record you need

If you cannot tell why VA decided the case the way it did, obtain the relevant records. VA Form 20-10206 is used for certain Privacy Act/records requests, including requests for a claims file. A claims-file request is not itself a review request and does not stop a review deadline.

  • Request early when the file is necessary to identify examination reports or prior evidence.
  • Do not wait for the C-file if doing so would cause you to miss a review deadline.
  • Keep your own index of decisions, exams, submissions, and receipts.
📋 CHECKLIST

Proof you should keep

  • VA decision notice and review-rights notice
  • Complete copy of the form and every attachment
  • VA.gov submission confirmation
  • Certified-mail receipt if mailed
  • Fax transmission receipt if faxed
  • Any delivery or intake confirmation
  • A dated log of follow-up calls

If the next decision is still wrong

  • After a Supplemental Claim decision: HLR, Board Appeal, or another Supplemental Claim may be available if requirements are met.
  • After HLR: Supplemental Claim or Board Appeal may be available; another HLR on the same issue is generally not.
  • After a Board decision: Supplemental Claim with new and relevant evidence or an appeal to the U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims may be available. CAVC appeals generally use a 120-day period from the Board decision.

🚨 GET A LAWYER Strongly consider an accredited veterans-law attorney for a CAVC appeal, complex effective-date dispute, contested claim, or procedural reduction/severance problem.

YOUR NEXT ACTION: Put the decision notice in front of you, mark the deadline, identify the exact reason for the decision, and use the prescribed review form.
