APPEALS NAVIGATION

When VA denies a claim or gives a lower rating than you expected, you have three main lanes to challenge the decision. Choose the right lane based on what went wrong and what evidence you have.

Deadline Calculator: When Do I Have to Act?

For most VA decisions, you have one year from the decision date to appeal or the decision becomes final. For Supplemental Claims filed after a final decision, there is no time limit, but you lose the earliest possible effective date if you wait.

Step 1: Find the date on the first page of your VA decision letter (usually top right). Step 2: Add one year. Step 3: File before that date to preserve your effective date. If the deadline falls on a weekend or federal holiday, file the previous business day.

How to Request Your C-File (VA Form 20-10206)

Your C-file contains every document VA has about you: claims, decisions, exams, service records, and correspondence. You need it to find errors and build an appeal. [23]

  1. Download and complete VA Form 20-10206.
  2. Check the box for "Claims File (C-File)" and any other records.
  3. Mail to the VA Evidence Intake Center or upload via VA.gov.
  4. Allow several weeks to months for delivery, especially for large files.
  5. Review the C-file with a VSO or attorney before choosing an appeal lane.

Sample Appeal Arguments for Common Denial Reasons

Denial reason: "No in-service event"

"STRs at pages [X-Y] document complaints of [symptom] during service. Buddy statements from [names] confirm the in-service injury. The veteran's current [diagnosis] is consistent with the in-service event. A new nexus opinion from Dr. [Name] is attached."

Denial reason: "No nexus"

"Attached is a nexus letter from [specialist] dated [date], which states it is at least as likely as not that [condition] is related to [in-service event]. The examiner reviewed the veteran's records and provides a reasoned medical rationale."

Denial reason: "Condition not chronic"

"The veteran's post-service treatment records from [provider] show continuous complaints and treatment for [condition] from [date] to present. The condition is chronic and ongoing."

How to Submit New Evidence in Each Lane

  • Supplemental Claim (20-0995): Attach new and relevant evidence with the form. Evidence must be new (not previously submitted) and relevant (relates to the issue). [19]
  • Higher-Level Review (20-0996): No new evidence allowed. Request an informal conference to explain the error. [20]
  • Board Appeal — Direct Review (10182): No new evidence; judge reviews the existing record. [21]
  • Board Appeal — Evidence Submission: Submit new evidence within 90 days after filing the Notice of Disagreement.
  • Board Appeal — Hearing: You can present testimony and evidence at the hearing.

Remand, Grant, or Denial

  • Grant: VA or the Board agrees. You receive a new rating and/or effective date and retroactive pay.
  • Denial: The decision is upheld. You may file a Supplemental Claim with new evidence or appeal further if a lane is available.
  • Remand: The Board sends the case back to VA for more development (new exam, missing records). This is common and usually delays the final decision but can lead to a grant.
YOUR NEXT ACTION: Read your decision letter. Identify the deadline. If you have new evidence, file a Supplemental Claim (20-0995). If you think VA made a clear error, file Higher-Level Review (20-0996). Do not miss the one-year deadline.
