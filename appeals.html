Fight a VA Denial: Pick the Right Appeal Lane

988 · 1 1-877-4AID-VET 1-800-799-SAFE

APPEALS NAVIGATION

A VA denial is not final. You have one year to choose an appeal lane, submit the right form, and force VA to look again. The lane you pick determines how fast it moves and whether you can add new evidence.

What This Is and Who Qualifies

A VA appeal is a formal decision review you can use when you disagree with a rating decision, denial, or effective date. You have the right to choose among three review lanes. [78]

Who qualifies: You must generally act within one year of the date on your decision letter. Contested claims have a 60-day deadline for Board Appeals. You cannot request a Higher-Level Review after another Higher-Level Review or Board Appeal on the same issue. [78] [80]

Three Lanes: Pick the Right One

LaneUse WhenNew Evidence?Form / How
Supplemental Claim [19] You have new and relevant evidence, or a change in law such as the PACT Act applies. Yes — submit or identify new evidence. VA Form 20-0995. Mail to the Claims Intake Center or submit online if eligible. [79]
Higher-Level Review [20] You believe VA made an error or you want a fresh look; no new evidence. No new evidence. VA Form 20-0996. Disability compensation can be filed online; other benefit types by mail. [79] [78]
Board Appeal [21] You want a Veterans Law Judge to review your case. Depends on type: Direct Review = no; Evidence Submission = yes within 90 days; Hearing = yes at hearing or within 90 days after. VA Form 10182. Choose Direct Review, Evidence Submission, or Hearing. [80]

Switching lanes: If VA has not yet decided your Supplemental Claim or Higher-Level Review, you can switch to another option within one year of the original decision letter. For Board Appeals, you can switch only if you have not yet submitted new evidence or had a hearing and the Board has not decided. [81]

Exact Steps to File Each Lane

Supplemental Claim (VA Form 20-0995)

  1. Gather new and relevant evidence: medical reports, buddy statements, treatment records, or a change-in-law basis (such as PACT Act presumption). [78]
  2. Download and complete VA Form 20-0995. [19]
  3. Attach the evidence and list any VA or private facilities you want VA to request records from, with treatment dates. [78]
  4. Submit online through VA.gov if eligible, or mail to:
    Department of Veterans Affairs
    Claims Intake Center
    PO Box 4444
    Janesville, WI 53547
    Phone: 1-800-827-1000 [79]
  5. Keep a copy of everything you send and get a tracking number or confirmation receipt.

Higher-Level Review (VA Form 20-0996)

  1. Make sure you are within one year of the decision letter and have no new evidence to submit. [78]
  2. Download and complete VA Form 20-0996. [20]
  3. For disability compensation, submit online at VA.gov. For other benefits, mail to the address on the form matching your benefit type. [79]
  4. You can request an informal conference with the reviewer; note the best phone number and time of day on the form.

Board Appeal (VA Form 10182)

  1. Choose your Board Appeal type:
    • Direct Review: No new evidence, no hearing. Goal: decision within 365 days. [80]
    • Evidence Submission: Submit new evidence with the appeal or within 90 days of VA receiving your request. Goal: decision within 550 days. [80]
    • Hearing: Meet with a Veterans Law Judge; submit evidence at the hearing or within 90 days after. [80]
  2. Download and complete VA Form 10182. [21]
  3. Mail the completed form to the address printed on the form. Keep copies and tracking confirmation.
  4. If you selected Evidence Submission or Hearing, calendar the 90-day evidence deadline.

Deadline Warnings: Protect Your Effective Date

  • One-year rule: For most decisions, you have one year from the date on the decision letter to file a Supplemental Claim, Higher-Level Review, or Board Appeal. [78]
  • Protective claim: Even a short signed letter saying “I intend to appeal the [date] decision on [condition]” can protect your effective date while you finish the official form. [22] [78]
  • Contested claims: Board Appeals may need to be filed within 60 days of the decision letter. [80]

What If They Say No

  • Supplemental Claim denied: You can file another Supplemental Claim with new evidence, request a Higher-Level Review, or request a Board Appeal if still within one year. [78]
  • Higher-Level Review denied: You can file a Supplemental Claim with new evidence or request a Board Appeal. [78]
  • Board Appeal denied: You may appeal to the U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (CAVC). You generally have 120 days from the Board decision. Get a VA-accredited attorney. [13]
  • No decision yet: Call VA at 1-800-827-1000 (TTY: 711). Do not file a duplicate review. [81]

Deadline Protection: Do Not Miss One Year

For most VA decisions, you have one year from the decision date to file a Supplemental Claim, Higher-Level Review, or Notice of Disagreement. Miss the deadline and the decision becomes final.

Step 1: Find the date on the first page of your VA decision letter. Step 2: Add one year. Step 3: File before that date. If the deadline falls on a weekend or federal holiday, file the previous business day. Use certified mail or VA.gov so you have proof.

Protective claims: Even a short letter saying "I intend to appeal the [date] decision on [condition]" can preserve your deadline if received before the one-year mark.

Get Your C-File (VA Form 20-10206)

Your C-file contains every document VA has about you. You need it to find errors and build an appeal. [23]

  1. Download VA Form 20-10206.
  2. Check the box for "Claims File (C-File)" and any other records.
  3. Mail to the VA Evidence Intake Center or upload via VA.gov.
  4. Allow several weeks to months for delivery.
  5. Review the C-file with a VSO or attorney before choosing an appeal lane.

Sample Appeal Arguments

Inadequate C&P exam:

"The [date] C&P exam is inadequate because the examiner failed to review [specific records], did not perform [specific test], and did not explain how [medical finding] affects [condition]. A new examination is required."

Benefit of the doubt: [75]

"The evidence is in approximate balance. Under 38 U.S.C. 5107(b), VA may need to resolve reasonable doubt in the veteran's favor. The in-service event, current diagnosis, and attached lay statements support service connection."

Staged ratings: [76]

"The veteran's symptoms have worsened since the original rating. A staged rating is warranted to reflect the severity of [condition] during the period from [date] to [date], supported by [treatment records / exams]."

Secondary conditions:

"The veteran's [secondary condition] was caused by the service-connected [primary condition], as confirmed by Dr. [Name] in the attached nexus letter. Service connection on a secondary basis is warranted."

Duty to assist: [37]

"Under 38 C.F.R. 3.159, VA has a duty to assist in obtaining identified relevant records. VA did not obtain [specific records] that the veteran identified. The case should be remanded for development."

New Evidence Rules Per Lane

  • Supplemental Claim (20-0995): Attach new and relevant evidence. Evidence may need to be new (not previously submitted) and relevant (relates to the issue). [19]
  • Higher-Level Review (20-0996): No new evidence allowed. Request an informal conference to explain the error. [20]
  • Board — Direct Review (10182): No new evidence; judge reviews the existing record. [21]
  • Board — Evidence Submission: Submit new evidence within 90 days after filing the NOD.
  • Board — Hearing: Present testimony and evidence at the hearing.

Remand, Grant, or Denial

  • Grant: VA or the Board agrees. You receive a new rating/effective date and retroactive pay.
  • Denial: The decision is upheld. You may file a Supplemental Claim with new evidence or appeal further if a lane remains.
  • Remand: The Board sends the case back to VA for more development. Common. Delays final decision but can lead to a grant.

Records to Keep (Appeals Tracker)

  • VA decision letter and rating code sheet
  • Copies of submitted appeal forms (20-0995, 20-0996, 10182)
  • Certified mail receipts or VA.gov confirmations
  • New evidence submitted and date submitted
  • C-file contents index
  • Call log with VA and VSO
