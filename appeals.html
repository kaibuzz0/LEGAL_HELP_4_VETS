APPEALS NAVIGATION

Denied? Do Not Stop.

A VA denial is not the end. It is the beginning of the next phase. You have options.

Three Lanes After a Decision

LaneWhen to UseWhat It DoesDeadline
Supplemental ClaimYou have new and relevant evidence.Reopens the issue with new evidence.1 year from decision for effective date protection.
Higher-Level ReviewYou believe VA made a mistake and you do not have new evidence.A senior reviewer re-examines the same record.1 year.
Board AppealYou want a Veterans Law Judge to review your case.Full appellate review. Evidence can be added at certain stages.1 year.

Appeals Strategy

  1. Read the rating decision's Reasons and Bases section.
  2. Identify exactly why VA denied or rated lower than expected.
  3. Choose the lane that matches your situation.
  4. File before the deadline.
  5. Request a copy of your C-file (complete claims file) with VA Form 20-10206.

Common Appeal Arguments

Inadequate C&P Exam

The exam was too short, ignored records, used an unqualified examiner, or contradicted medical evidence. Request a new exam or submit an independent medical opinion.

Benefit of the Doubt

Under 38 C.F.R. § 3.102, when the evidence is roughly equal, the veteran wins. Argue this when the record supports your claim but VA chose against it.

Staged Ratings

If your condition got worse over time, you may be entitled to separate ratings for separate time periods. This can increase back pay.

Secondary Conditions

A service-connected condition caused or worsened another condition. File separately for each secondary condition with a nexus opinion.

Duty to Assist

VA must help get relevant records. If VA failed to obtain records, request a remand or supplemental development.

Appeals Deadlines

  • 1 year from the date of the rating decision to appeal or file a supplemental claim for effective date protection.
  • Missing the deadline does not always end your case, but it may cost you months or years of back pay.
