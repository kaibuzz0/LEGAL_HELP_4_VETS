I NEED HELP NOW

This section is for veterans and families in immediate crisis or facing collapse within days. It tells you who to call, what to say, and what to do next.

If You Have 1 Hour Today

  1. Put the crisis number in your phone: 988 press 1. [1]
  2. Text or call one person who knows your situation and can check on you.
  3. Identify the single most urgent problem from the table above and make one call about it.
  4. Write down the name, phone number, and case number of whoever you talked to.
  5. If you are unsafe to drive or alone, get to a public place, VA emergency room, or fire station.

If you have more time, use the other pages on this site for the full plan: housing, claims, appeals, substance use, widows, employment and money.

Crisis Phone Numbers

  • Veterans Crisis Line: Call 988, then press 1, or text 838255. Confidential, 24/7, free. [1]
  • National Call Center for Homeless Veterans: 1-877-4AID-VET (1-877-424-3838), 24/7. [32]
  • Domestic Violence / MST: 1-800-799-SAFE (7233) or 1-425-538-1856 (MST text).

What to Say When You Call 1-877-4AID-VET (Homeless Veteran Hotline)

"My name is [name]. I am a veteran [or family member of veteran [name]]. I am homeless / about to lose my housing in [city, state] within [timeframe]. I need emergency housing assistance and a local SSVF referral. Can you connect me to the nearest VA homeless coordinator?"

Information to have ready: your name, city/county, discharge status if known, income sources, household size, whether you have minor children, whether you are fleeing domestic violence.

Housing: SSVF and HUD-VASH Step-by-Step

SSVF (Supportive Services for Veteran Families) provides short-term help to avoid homelessness or leave homelessness. [30]

  1. Call 1-877-4AID-VET or find your local SSVF provider at the VA homeless program locator.
  2. Tell them you need SSVF intake. Ask for an appointment and the required documents list.
  3. Gather before the appointment: DD-214, photo ID, proof of income (last 30 days), lease/eviction notice or statement of homelessness, bank statements, utility bills, household member IDs, and a written budget.
  4. Attend intake. Be honest about debts, barriers, and medical needs.
  5. If approved, ask exactly what costs SSVF can cover (rent, utilities, security deposit, moving costs, childcare, transportation) and for how long.

HUD-VASH is a long-term housing voucher plus VA case management, mainly for chronically homeless veterans. [31]

  1. Contact your local VA homeless program or call 1-877-4AID-VET.
  2. Complete a VA housing assessment and screening.
  3. If referred, work with a VA case manager to obtain a HUD-VASH voucher.
  4. Use the voucher to lease a unit. The case manager continues support.

Emergency Document Checklist (Priority Order)

If you have to flee or move fast, grab these in this order:

  1. ID and DD-214 (or any discharge paperwork)
  2. VA health card / Veteran ID Card
  3. Social Security card and birth certificate
  4. Medications and prescription list
  5. Phone and charger, cash, debit/credit cards
  6. Lease, eviction notice, utility bills, pay stubs if you have them
  7. Contact list: family, VSO, employer, doctor

Medical Crisis: VA Urgent Care vs. ER

  • Life-threatening: Call 911 or go to the nearest ER. VA may reimburse emergency care in non-VA facilities if criteria are met.
  • Urgent but not life-threatening: Use a VA urgent care clinic or in-network community urgent care (requires VA enrollment/eligibility). [4]
  • VA care complaint: Contact the Patient Advocate at your VA medical center. [40]

Food and Money While You Stabilize

  • SNAP (food stamps): Apply through your state human services office. Veterans with low income and dependents often qualify. [34]
  • SSDI/SSI: If you cannot work due to a medical condition expected to last 12+ months or result in death, file with Social Security. [33]
  • VA pension: Wartime veterans with limited income and permanent disability may qualify for VA Pension.
  • TANF / cash assistance: State-run. Apply at the same office that handles SNAP.

SSVF can cover: past-due rent, security deposits, utility deposits/arrears, moving costs, emergency supplies, and transportation — but only what your local SSVF program has funding for. Verify locally.

Records to Keep (Call Log Template)

Every call, save this information in a notebook or notes app:

  • Date and time
  • Agency / person / phone number / extension
  • What you asked for
  • What they said (verbatim if possible)
  • Case number, reference number, or next steps
  • Deadline or follow-up date
