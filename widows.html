WIDOWS AND SURVIVING SPOUSES

Losing a servicemember or veteran is devastating. This section tells surviving spouses, children, and parents what benefits may be available, how to file, and what documents you need.

What This Is and Who Qualifies

Surviving spouses, dependent children, and sometimes dependent parents of veterans may be entitled to monthly compensation, health care, education benefits, home loan eligibility, and burial honors. This page focuses on the most common federal survivor benefits. [7] [8]

Who qualifies: Eligibility depends on the veteran’s service, cause of death, your relationship, and whether you remarried. Many programs require the surviving spouse to have been married to the veteran for a minimum period or to have a child with the veteran. [7] [9] [10]

DIC (Dependency and Indemnity Compensation)

DIC pays a monthly tax-free benefit to eligible surviving spouses and dependent children when a veteran’s death resulted from a service-connected condition, or when the veteran was totally service-connected disabled for 10 years before death. [7]

  1. Check eligibility: Death must be service-connected, OR the veteran was rated totally disabled (including TDIU) for 10 years before death. Some exceptions apply for veterans rated totally disabled for less than 10 years. [7]
  2. Complete VA Form 21P-534EZ (Application for DIC, Survivors Pension, and/or Accrued Benefits). [46]
  3. Attach: marriage certificate, veteran’s death certificate, veteran’s DD-214, birth certificates for dependent children, and any medical records linking death to service.
  4. Submit: Online, by mail, in person, or through a VSO. Use certified mail if mailing.
  5. Effective date: DIC is generally effective the first day of the month the claim is received, or the date of death if the claim is filed within one year of death. Filing within one year also preserves the earliest possible effective date. [7]

SBP (Survivor Benefit Plan) and DIC Offset

The Survivor Benefit Plan (SBP) is a military retirement annuity. DFAS pays it to eligible survivors of servicemembers and retirees. If you also receive DIC, SBP may be offset (reduced) by the DIC amount, though recent laws have reduced or eliminated the offset for some survivors. [8]

  1. Who gets SBP: Spouse or former spouse named in the SBP election, and in some cases children. Check the military retiree’s SBP election at DFAS. [8]
  2. How to claim: Contact DFAS at 1-800-321-1080 and request the SBP annuity. Have the retiree’s Social Security number, your marriage certificate, and the death certificate ready. [8]
  3. DIC offset: If you receive both SBP and DIC, SBP may be reduced by the DIC amount. Ask DFAS for a current calculation. Laws change; verify the current offset rules. [8]
  4. Death gratuity / arrears: Ask DFAS about any unpaid retired pay or arrears of pay due to the estate.

CHAMPVA: Health Care for Survivors and Dependents

CHAMPVA provides health coverage for eligible surviving spouses and dependent children of veterans who died from a service-connected disability, or who were rated totally disabled from service-connected conditions. [9] [53]

  1. Check eligibility: You must be the spouse or child of a veteran who died from a service-connected condition, or who was totally disabled from service-connected conditions at time of death. [9]
  2. Apply: Complete VA Form 10-10d (Application for CHAMPVA Benefits). [48]
  3. Attach: marriage certificate, birth certificates for children, death certificate, Social Security numbers, and proof of other health insurance if any.
  4. Mail to:
    VA Health Administration Center
    CHAMPVA
    PO Box 65024
    Denver, CO 80206-9024
    Phone: 1-800-733-8387 [9]

You cannot use Fry and DEA for the same period. Choose the one that pays the most for your situation. [10] [11]

VA Home Loan for Surviving Spouses

Some surviving spouses can use the veteran’s VA home loan entitlement to buy, build, or refinance a home with no down payment and no private mortgage insurance. [12] [44]

  1. Check eligibility: Generally, an unmarried surviving spouse of a servicemember who died in service or of a service-connected disability, or a spouse of a missing-in-action/prisoner-of-war servicemember. [12]
  2. Request Certificate of Eligibility (COE): Apply online at VA.gov or complete VA Form 26-1880. [44]
  3. Attach: marriage certificate, death certificate, and veteran’s DD-214.
  4. Find a VA-approved lender and provide your COE. [12]

Burial Benefits and Honors

VA provides burial benefits including a burial allowance, plot or interment allowance, headstone or marker, burial flag, and Presidential Memorial Certificate. [56]

  • Apply for burial allowance with VA Form 21P-530EZ (or online). [56]
  • Request a headstone, marker, or medallion through the VA National Cemetery Administration. [56]
  • Request a United States burial flag through a VA regional office or U.S. Post Office. [56]
  • Request a Presidential Memorial Certificate online or by mail. [56]
  • Arrange military funeral honors through the funeral home or the veteran’s branch of service.

How to Find an Accredited VSO, Attorney, or Agent

Survivor claims are complex and deadlines matter. Accredited representatives work free of charge or for limited fees approved by VA. [13] [57]

  1. Search the VA Office of General Counsel accreditation database for an accredited attorney, claims agent, or VSO representative. [57]
  2. Contact a Veterans Service Organization (VFW, DAV, American Legion, Vietnam Veterans of America, etc.) and ask for a representative who handles survivor claims. [13]
  3. Ask the representative: “Have you handled DIC, CHAMPVA, Fry Scholarship, and SBP claims in the last year?”
  4. Sign a VA Form 21-22 or 21-22a so the representative can access your file.

Single Mothers and Dependent Children

Children of a deceased veteran may qualify for benefits even if the surviving parent does not remarry or does not qualify for DIC. [7] [9] [60]

  • DIC for children: Unmarried dependent children under age 18 (or 23 if in school) may receive DIC if the veteran’s death was service-connected. [7]
  • CHAMPVA for children: Children of a veteran who died from service-connected causes or who was totally disabled may qualify. [9]
  • Education benefits: Fry Scholarship and DEA Chapter 35 can be used by dependent children. [10] [11]
  • Social Security survivor benefits: Contact SSA at 1-800-772-1213 or ssa.gov. Children may qualify if the veteran had enough work credits. [60]

Document Checklist for Survivor Claims

  • Marriage certificate (and any prior marriage/divorce records)
  • Veteran’s death certificate
  • Veteran’s DD-214 (all copies)
  • Birth certificates for all dependent children
  • Social Security numbers for spouse and children
  • Dependency records (adoption, stepchild, or dependent parent proof if applicable)
  • Medical records linking cause of death to service (for DIC)
  • Copies of all applications submitted, with dates and tracking numbers

What If They Say No

  • DIC denied: File a Supplemental Claim with new medical evidence linking death to service, or request a Higher-Level Review or Board Appeal. [19] [20] [21]
  • CHAMPVA denied: Appeal through the Health Administration Center in writing. Verify dependency documents are complete. [9]
  • SBP problems: Contact DFAS and request a written explanation of the offset calculation. [8]
  • Education benefit denied: Contact VA Education at 1-888-442-4551 and ask for a written denial with appeal rights. [55]

DIC (Dependency and Indemnity Compensation)

DIC is a tax-free monthly payment for surviving spouses, children, and parents when a servicemember dies in the line of duty or a veteran's death resulted from a service-connected condition. [7]

Who qualifies: Surviving spouses who were married to the veteran at the time of death and were not remarried before age 55 (or at all); dependent children under 18, or 18-23 if in school; and dependent parents in some cases.

  1. Gather documents: marriage certificate, veteran's death certificate, veteran's DD-214, birth certificates for children, and Social Security numbers.
  2. File VA Form 21P-534EZ. <[46]/li>
  3. If the veteran had a pending claim for the condition that caused death, include the claim number and a statement that the cause of death was service-connected.
  4. Submit online at VA.gov, by mail to the Evidence Intake Center, or through a VSO.

Effective date: Generally the first day of the month after the veteran's death if filed within one year; otherwise the month after VA receives the claim.

SBP (Survivor Benefit Plan)

SBP is a DOD annuity that pays a monthly benefit to eligible survivors of retired servicemembers who elected and paid for the plan. <[8]/p>

  • Who gets it: The beneficiary the retiree designated (usually spouse or spouse and children).
  • SBP-DIC offset: SBP payments may be reduced dollar-for-dollar by DIC payments in some cases. DFAS administers the offset. Request an SBP-DIC audit if you believe the offset is incorrect.
  • How to claim: Contact DFAS and the retiree's service branch personnel center. Report the death and submit the death certificate and beneficiary documentation.

CHAMPVA

CHAMPVA is comprehensive health coverage for qualifying spouses and children of veterans rated permanently and totally disabled or who died from a service-connected condition. [53]p>

  1. Confirm eligibility: spouse or dependent child of a P&T-rated or service-connected-death veteran.
  2. Apply online or by mail using the CHAMPVA application. Attach marriage certificate, birth certificates, and the veteran's rating/decision letter or death certificate.
  3. Once enrolled, use CHAMPVA-authorized providers. You may also have Medicare or other insurance as secondary/coordination of benefits.

Both programs provide education benefits to survivors and dependents, but they have different eligibility rules. [10]s[11]p>

VA Home Loan for Surviving Spouses

Some surviving spouses may be eligible for a VA-backed home loan with no down payment and no PMI. [51]p>

  1. Confirm eligibility: generally unremarried surviving spouses of servicemembers who died in service or of veterans with service-connected death.
  2. Request a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) through VA.gov or a lender. [44]li>
  3. Apply with a VA-approved lender. The lender will order the COE if you do not have it.
  4. Provide the lender with the veteran's DD-214 and death certificate.

Burial Benefits

VA provides burial benefits for eligible veterans. [56]p>

  • National cemetery burial: Gravesite, opening/closing, vault/liner, headstone or marker, flag, and Presidential Memorial Certificate.
  • Burial allowance: A partial reimbursement for burial/funeral costs if the death was service-connected or the veteran was receiving certain VA benefits.
  • How to claim: Contact the National Cemetery Scheduling Office or your funeral director to coordinate. File VA Form 21P-530EZ for burial allowance.

Find a VA-Accredited Representative or VSO

Survivor benefit claims can be complex. A VA-accredited representative can help at no upfront cost. [57]p>

Script: "I am the surviving spouse of [veteran name]. He/she passed away on [date]. I need help filing for DIC, CHAMPVA, and education benefits. Can you represent me or review my paperwork?"

For Single Mothers and Dependent Children

If the veteran was your spouse and you have dependent children, additional benefits may apply:

  • CHAMPVA: Children may be eligible until age 18 (or longer if a full-time student or disabled before 18). [53]li>
  • Education benefits: Fry Scholarship or DEA (Chapter 35). Apply at VA.gov education section. [47]li>
  • Social Security survivor benefits: Children under 18 and surviving spouses caring for young children may qualify. Contact SSA. [60]li>
  • DIC for children: Unmarried dependent children may receive DIC directly in some cases. Include their birth certificates in the application.

Document Checklist for Survivor Benefits

  • Veteran's DD-214
  • Marriage certificate
  • Veteran's death certificate
  • Birth certificates for all dependent children
  • Social Security numbers for spouse and children
  • VA decision letters showing service-connected ratings or pending claims
  • Dependency records or school enrollment for children over 18
YOUR NEXT ACTION: If the veteran died recently, gather the marriage certificate, death certificate, DD-214, and children's birth certificates. Then contact a VSO or file VA Form 21P-534EZ for DIC and CHAMPVA within the first year to protect the earliest effective date.
