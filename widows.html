MILITARY WIDOWS & SURVIVING SPOUSES

Losing a service member or veteran is devastating. These benefits and legal pathways exist to help survivors stabilize, get health care, finish school, and keep a roof overhead.

Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC)

DIC is a monthly, tax-free benefit for surviving spouses and children when a veteran's death resulted from a service-connected condition. [7]

Survivor Benefit Plan (SBP)

SBP is a Department of Defense annuity that can provide monthly income to survivors of military retirees who elected coverage. [8]

Health Care for Survivors

CHAMPVA provides health coverage for eligible survivors who cannot use TRICARE, including surviving spouses and dependent children of veterans with certain service-connected disabilities or deaths. [9]

Education Benefits

The Fry Scholarship provides Post-9/11 GI Bill benefits to children and spouses of service members who died in the line of duty after September 10, 2001. [10] Dependents' Educational Assistance (DEA, Chapter 35) provides education benefits to dependents of veterans with certain service-connected disabilities or deaths. [11]

VA Home Loan Options for Survivors

Surviving spouses may have options regarding an existing VA-guaranteed home loan, including assumptions or refinancing in some cases. [12]

Getting Help — VSOs and Legal Aid

VA-accredited representatives and Veterans Service Organizations can help survivors file DIC claims, appeal denials, and navigate benefit elections. [13]

For Single-Mother Widows

Dependency rules, custody status, and the child's age can affect DIC and education benefits. A VSO or legal aid attorney can help ensure children receive every benefit for which they are eligible.

YOUR NEXT ACTION: Start with a VSO to file a DIC claim if the death may be service-connected, and request a benefits summary from the VA to identify all survivor benefits available.
