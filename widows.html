WIDOWS AND SURVIVING SPOUSES

Losing a servicemember or veteran is devastating. This section tells surviving spouses, children, and parents what benefits may be available, how to file, and what documents you need.

DIC (Dependency and Indemnity Compensation)

DIC is a tax-free monthly payment for surviving spouses, children, and parents when a servicemember dies in the line of duty or a veteran's death resulted from a service-connected condition. [53]

Who qualifies: Surviving spouses who were married to the veteran at the time of death and were not remarried before age 55 (or at all); dependent children under 18, or 18-23 if in school; and dependent parents in some cases.

  1. Gather documents: marriage certificate, veteran's death certificate, veteran's DD-214, birth certificates for children, and Social Security numbers.
  2. File VA Form 21P-534EZ. [46]
  3. If the veteran had a pending claim for the condition that caused death, include the claim number and a statement that the cause of death was service-connected.
  4. Submit online at VA.gov, by mail to the Evidence Intake Center, or through a VSO.

Effective date: Generally the first day of the month after the veteran's death if filed within one year; otherwise the month after VA receives the claim.

SBP (Survivor Benefit Plan)

SBP is a DOD annuity that pays a monthly benefit to eligible survivors of retired servicemembers who elected and paid for the plan. [61]

  • Who gets it: The beneficiary the retiree designated (usually spouse or spouse and children).
  • SBP-DIC offset: SBP payments may be reduced dollar-for-dollar by DIC payments in some cases. DFAS administers the offset. Request an SBP-DIC audit if you believe the offset is incorrect.
  • How to claim: Contact DFAS and the retiree's service branch personnel center. Report the death and submit the death certificate and beneficiary documentation.

CHAMPVA

CHAMPVA is comprehensive health coverage for qualifying spouses and children of veterans rated permanently and totally disabled or who died from a service-connected condition. [54]

  1. Confirm eligibility: spouse or dependent child of a P&T-rated or service-connected-death veteran.
  2. Apply online or by mail using the CHAMPVA application. Attach marriage certificate, birth certificates, and the veteran's rating/decision letter or death certificate.
  3. Once enrolled, use CHAMPVA-authorized providers. You may also have Medicare or other insurance as secondary/coordination of benefits.

Fry Scholarship vs. DEA (Chapter 35)

Both programs provide education benefits to survivors and dependents, but they have different eligibility rules. [10] [11]

ProgramWho QualifiesDeadlines / Notes
Fry ScholarshipChildren and surviving spouses of servicemembers who died in the line of duty after 9/10/2001.Children generally must use benefits between ages 18 and 26, with exceptions. Spouses lose eligibility if they remarry. [55]
DEA (Chapter 35)Children and spouses of veterans rated P&T disabled or who died from a service-connected condition.Children must generally use benefits between ages 18 and 26. Apply with VA Form 22-5490. [47]

Important: Children eligible for both Fry Scholarship and DEA can use one program at a time. Plan which to use based on benefit rates and remaining entitlement.

VA Home Loan for Surviving Spouses

Some surviving spouses may be eligible for a VA-backed home loan with no down payment and no PMI. [51]

  1. Confirm eligibility: generally unremarried surviving spouses of servicemembers who died in service or of veterans with service-connected death.
  2. Request a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) through VA.gov or a lender. [44]
  3. Apply with a VA-approved lender. The lender will order the COE if you do not have it.
  4. Provide the lender with the veteran's DD-214 and death certificate.

Burial Benefits

VA provides burial benefits for eligible veterans. [57]

  • National cemetery burial: Gravesite, opening/closing, vault/liner, headstone or marker, flag, and Presidential Memorial Certificate.
  • Burial allowance: A partial reimbursement for burial/funeral costs if the death was service-connected or the veteran was receiving certain VA benefits.
  • How to claim: Contact the National Cemetery Scheduling Office or your funeral director to coordinate. File VA Form 21P-530EZ for burial allowance.

Find a VA-Accredited Representative or VSO

Survivor benefit claims can be complex. A VA-accredited representative can help at no upfront cost. [58]

Script: "I am the surviving spouse of [veteran name]. He/she passed away on [date]. I need help filing for DIC, CHAMPVA, and education benefits. Can you represent me or review my paperwork?"

For Single Mothers and Dependent Children

If the veteran was your spouse and you have dependent children, additional benefits may apply:

  • CHAMPVA: Children may be eligible until age 18 (or longer if a full-time student or disabled before 18). [54]
  • Education benefits: Fry Scholarship or DEA (Chapter 35). Apply at VA.gov education section. [47]
  • Social Security survivor benefits: Children under 18 and surviving spouses caring for young children may qualify. Contact SSA. [62]
  • DIC for children: Unmarried dependent children may receive DIC directly in some cases. Include their birth certificates in the application.

Document Checklist for Survivor Benefits

  • Veteran's DD-214
  • Marriage certificate
  • Veteran's death certificate
  • Birth certificates for all dependent children
  • Social Security numbers for spouse and children
  • VA decision letters showing service-connected ratings or pending claims
  • Dependency records or school enrollment for children over 18
YOUR NEXT ACTION: If the veteran died recently, gather the marriage certificate, death certificate, DD-214, and children's birth certificates. Then contact a VSO or file VA Form 21P-534EZ for DIC and CHAMPVA within the first year to protect the earliest effective date.
